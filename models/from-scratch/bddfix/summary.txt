  - B': no starved copies. At most one R' node at any checkpoint sends >= 96% to one sub-router (bddregret's B: 4 of 7

  - C is the likely cause of the damage. A' only decides when to split and B' only changes what R' trains on, but it
    is the copies that got worse. This is not tested: the drops were to run only if the combined arm won.

WHAT WAS TRIED, ACROSS THE FOUR STUDIES
The idea: a router that stops improving splits like a leaf. A new router R' sits over three copies of the old router
(sub-routers), all routing to the old router's children. The copies can then specialize, and R' learns which copy to
use where.
1. bddcheck, as built: one seed, one pass, split limit 5. Level with its BDT twin, -0.0018 [-0.0043, +0.0006], both
   with 7 leaves. Deployed as D-L-Ne-c.
2. bddstudy: 60 rounds, two seeds, split limit 10, the setup for every later test.
   - Router splits took 6 of the 10 splits, and the root split 3-4 times.
   - The BDD tied a BDT with its 9 leaves: +0.0010 [-0.0002, +0.0021].
   - The copies diverged slowly, and R' did not learn to use them.
3. bddregret: two one-factor fixes.
   - A: split on routing regret instead of the router's log-loss.
   - B: train R' on the losses of the leaves its sub-routers reach.
   - Against the same-leaves BDT: -0.0008 to -0.0012, and no interval excludes zero.
   - A re-split new R' nodes: their regret starts at zero and can only rise. B sent almost everything to the first
     copy, because ties go to the first child.
4. bddfix (this run): A' + B' + C together, each aimed at one defect above. +0.0005 [-0.0011, +0.0020] at round 60,
   and worse at rounds 20 and 40.
Five variants, two seeds each. At round 60 against a BDT with the same leaves, the means run from -0.0012 to +0.0010,
and no interval excludes zero.

WHY ROUTER SPLITS DON'T HELP ON THIS DATA
1. The trigger fires on a rise that happens anyway.
   - Right after a leaf split, the new leaves are near-copies, so every routing choice is about equally good. The
     router's error and regret start low.
   - They climb as the leaves specialize, whether or not the router is learning. The split rule (Worse, margin 0)
     reads any climb as a stall.
   - In all 10 of the 60-round runs (5 variants x 2 seeds), the root's first router split came in round 4. That is
     the first round in which it had two closed windows to compare (structure.json: its first window closed in round 3).
   - Router splits then took 6-7 of each run's 10 splits.
   - Round to round, the test-set root regret of a BDT that never splits moves by up to 0.03 (0.180, 0.155, 0.164,
     0.185, 0.151 at rounds 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8, seed 1; drift.txt). A zero margin can't tell a stall from that.
2. A split adds no routing skill, and none appears later. R' starts from nothing over copies of the old router.
   - Copies that keep their counts stay alike (94-97% agreement at the end of the split's round) and change slowly,
     so R' has little to choose between.
   - Copies that forget (C) part quickly, but each ends up a worse router than the unsplit one would have been.
   - Either way, R' ends level with simply using its most-used sub-router. At round 60, no variant clearly beats that
     in both seeds (nats per test position, s1 / s2): BDD -0.0028 / +0.0056, A -0.0021 / -0.0020 (both intervals
     include zero), B +0.0000 / -0.0001, A+B -0.0003 / +0.0003, bddfix -0.0006 / -0.0008.
3. On this data, little is lost or gained.
   - Better routing among the same leaves has found little room here. Across the five variants at rounds 20, 40 and
     60, held-out log-loss against the same-leaves BDT ran from -0.0016 to +0.0031. The only intervals that exclude
     zero are bddfix's at rounds 20 and 40, and those are worse.
   - Spending splits on routers costs little: the 7-leaf BDT ties the 21-leaf BDT at round 60 (-0.0015 [-0.0037,
     +0.0007]).
   - So the DAG ties whichever BDT it is compared with. It has fewer leaves, and fewer leaves cost nothing here.
If router splits are revisited, the trigger is the part to change. One option is to keep the old router as a shadow
and split only if R' and its copies beat it over a window, as --revise does for leaves. I'd expect that to suppress
most router splits, making the BDD a BDT, rather than make it better.

THE OPTIONS
Binary bin/chessai-bddfix (md5 963e86ed), built from bddfix-src = bddregret-src plus bddfix.patch (237 changed lines).
- A', --delegator-split-on leaf-regret.
  - A delegator's window statistic is the loss of the leaf the position finally reaches, minus the best loss among
    all leaves reachable below the delegator.
  - So a new R' starts at its old router's regret. For a delegator over leaves it equals A's regret.
  - Split rule otherwise as A: the leaves' rule (Worse, split_z = 0 here).
- B', --subrouter-target leaf-skip.
  - As B, except that R' skips its update (routes, but does not train) where every sub-router reaches the same leaf.
  - Otherwise the Best target over the reached leaves' losses, with ties going to the first child.
- C, --router-forget-factor F (16 here; default: --forget-factor).
  - The copies made by a router split have their counts divided by F. Leaf-split copies keep --forget-factor (1).
- New params print in the state only when set, so old states stay byte-identical.
- Tests: 142 pass, including four new ones:
  - a state round trip with all three options;
  - leaf regret equals a hand computation against the leaves below;
  - R' skips positions where its sub-routers agree;
  - router-split copies are divided by F.
- Identity (identity.sh, identity.out): without the new options, chessai-bddfix retrains bddregret's bdda-s1 and
  bddab-s1 and bddstudy's bdd-s1 byte for byte over 8 rounds, through the root's router split in round 4. Its round-8
  state and train.log (timings removed) equal chessai-bddregret's.
