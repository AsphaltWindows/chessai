Topic 2 (2026-11-07/08 night): expected-loss router target in trees (--error-target eloss).

Verdict: as a training target it is clearly harmful, so there are no lines and no smoke.
- The routers rank the children better: the root's routing regret falls to 0.
- But because a router's pick also decides which child trains on the example, the regression routes each position to
  the child that is already best there, and specialization feeds itself.
- The leaves end up specialists on narrower data, and the tree is 0.0235 worse.
- The best-child target's noise was keeping the leaves generalists.
- What errwhy showed still stands: refitting routers with this target on frozen children gains. So the target belongs
  in a router used only for play, not in the one that assigns training examples (see "Next", below).

Code: ../eloss-src = ../sparsefit-src + eloss.patch (eloss-src/eloss.patch).
- ErrorTarget::Eloss: each child's error below the children's mean, regressed by the router's logits. Squared error,
  one AdaGrad step (Logreg::train_regress).
- Routing goes to the largest logit, i.e. the least predicted error.
- It trains every error router:
  - plain error routing (--router lr);
  - switch trees' error routers, while comparing and after the switch (--error-router lr with --route-switch);
  - split trials' routers.
- The router's recorded error is its log-loss on the best child, as with best targets.
- Graph::new refuses it for routers that are not LR.
- CLI doc updated. Three new unit tests:
  - eloss prefers the calibrated child where best prefers the overconfident one, and follows the mean error for
    absolute error;
  - it trains LR routers;
  - it refuses CNBP routers.
- 138 tests pass.

Routing on the test round (bin/chessai-eloss routing), le-eloss against le-100k:
- Root regret (against each group's best child): 0.0000, against 0.0028.
- Against each position's best child: 0.142, against 0.071.

  - le-eloss's children score 0.40, 0.51 and 0.43;
  - le-100k's score 0.44, 0.43 and 0.43.

Switch trees: no delegator of T-L-NLs's recipe ever switched to its LR error router in this benchmark, even at
--route-switch 0. The error router's mean gain over the cluster router was never positive. So the target had no
effect: the model files are identical. This doesn't test eloss in switch trees; it says their error routers lose.

T-L-Ne's recipe: CNBP routers can't regress, so eloss on that recipe would mean LR routers, i.e. le-eloss.

Next (idea, not built): an eloss play router.
- Each delegator would train a second LR router on every scored example with this target, and predict with it.
- The training routers would still assign the training examples, so the leaves stay generalists.
- Model files would carry the play routers in the routers' place, so the tournament binary is unchanged. State files
  keep both.
- errwhy B is the evidence: an LR regress router refitted on frozen children gained 0.013 on T-L-Ne-c and 0.0026 on
  T-L-Le-c.
- Offline, it is exactly paired: training is identical, only the test-time routing differs.
