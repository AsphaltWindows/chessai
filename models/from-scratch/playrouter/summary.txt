Idea (from errwhy and topic 2):
- errwhy B: LR routers refitted with an expected-loss target on frozen T-L-Ne-c children gained 0.013, 51% of the
  reachable gain.
- Topic 2: the same target is harmful as a training router, because the router's pick also decides who trains, and
  specialization feeds itself.
- So: keep the training router as it is, assigning training examples, and add a play router that only predicts.

Code: ../playrouter-src = ../blendplay-src + playrouter.patch (308 lines).
- New option --play-router, BDT only (DelegationParams::play_router, in the options string only when set).
- Each delegator gets an LR play router when it is made (Node::play_router). On every example that reaches it, the
  play router takes one regress step (Logreg::train_regress, as eloss) towards its children's expected-loss target:
  each child's 3-class log-loss below the children's mean.
  - Error-routed delegators reuse the children's log-losses they already computed. Cluster delegators compute them.
- Training is unchanged: the training routers still route the examples. In-process self-play plays with the
  training routers in both players (the previous version is an in-memory clone), so self-play is unchanged too.
- Saved model files hold the play routers in the routers' place. Since the text format is self-describing per node,
  a play-router model file is an ordinary BDT with LR routers, and any binary that reads LR-router trees plays it.
- State files keep both routers (a conditional section, so older states still load). forget() covers play routers.
- Refused with --revise, --split-trial and --leaf-residual (they replace or test nodes, which would need play-router
  handling).
- Three new unit tests:
  - play routers leave training unchanged (every leaf predicts the same as without) and are saved as the routers;
  - a play router prefers the calibrated child over the overconfident one (as eloss);
  - play routers refuse revisions.
- 142 tests pass. The first build failed to compile the tests (a stray field in a test literal of the old options;
  chain1.out, test1.log); fixed, test code only, before anything was installed.

- These are log-loss differences against the twin. "Bottom" means delegators whose children are all leaves: the
  positions reaching them come through training routers above, just as in training.
- T-L-Ne's damage is nearly all at the root. Elsewhere both placements lose.
- Two causes are likely:
  - Online targets: a play router regresses errors of children that change all through training (AdaGrad, no
    forgetting), whereas errwhy B refitted on frozen children.
  - Whole-tree replacement: errwhy B scored one delegator at a time with the others' own routers, while a root play
    router sends positions down subtrees that never trained on them.
