THE OPTIONS
Binary chessai-bddregret (md5 2b76d0e2), built from bddregret-src = bddstudy-src plus the two options. Both options
require --route error.
- A, --delegator-split-on regret (default router-error).
  - A delegator's window statistic is its prequential routing regret: the routed child's loss minus the best child's
    loss, by --error. The pick is the router's argmax before it trains on the position.
  - The regret goes into the node's window and into graph.router_errors.
  - It splits by the leaves' SplitRule logic and split_z. Under Worse (the default, used here) the condition is
    recent > previous + margin, and the "at least the mean of all routers" condition is dropped, as Worse has none
    for leaves either. Under StallHigh that condition is kept, as the mean regret of all delegators over the window.
  - An R''s "children" are its sub-routers, so its regret is measured against the leaves they reach.
- B, --subrouter-target leaf (default routing).
  - R' trains with normal error routing. Each sub-router's error is the loss of the leaf it would route to
    (prediction_errors, routing through delegator children by argmax), as a BDT router trains on its children.
  - The target is --error-target (Best here), with ties going to the first child.
- New fields print in the state's params only when non-default, so old states stay byte-identical.
- Tests: 139 passed, including the new ones:
  - state round trips with the options;
  - window sums equal probe-computed regrets;
  - R''s classifier equals a clone trained on error_target(prediction_errors);
  - delegators split on regret;
  - regret without error routing panics.
- Identity (identity.sh, identity.out):
  - Without the options, or with them spelled at their defaults, the binary retrains bddstudy's bdd-s1 and bdt-s1
    byte for byte over 8 rounds, through the root's router split at round 4. bdd8.state and train.log match
    chessai-cyc5's (timings removed).
  - bddb's round-3 models equal bddstudy's bdd-sN byte for byte.
  - bdt3-s2 at round 20 scores exactly as bdt4-s2 and bdt-s2 (the same model by then).
  - bdda's bdd-stats outputs are byte-identical to bdd's through round 5 (s1) and 6 (s2), where the two runs had made
    the same splits. This also checks the new binary's bdd-stats against chessai-bddstudy's.

SETUP
bddstudy's setup.
- Data: offline-c15, RRs 80-83 (2.31M positions) in 60 rounds (3 passes, bddstudy/train). The test set is RR 84 (575k
  positions, 10 parts).
- T-L-Ne-c's recipe: LR leaves, CNBP error routers, --error log, branch 3, forget 1, split threshold 100k, split limit
  10, seeds 1 and 2, nice 19, single-threaded, at most 2 jobs. One slip: for about 4 minutes, 4 jobs ran. A waiter
  keyed on /usr/bin/time's output file, which is created at start, so the two control jobs were paused (SIGSTOP)
  until the A+B runs finished.
- Arms (run.sh): bdda (A), bddb (B), bddab (A+B).
- Controls: bddstudy's bdd, bdt (limit 10) and bdt4 (limit 4, 9 leaves), all trained with chessai-cyc5, plus new
  bdt3 runs (run2.sh, limit 3, 7 leaves).
  - Router splits count against the split limit, so the arms ended with 7 leaves (bdda, bddab, bddb-s1) or 9 leaves
    (bddb-s2).
  - The same-leaves control is bdt3-sN for 7 leaves and bdt4-sN for 9.
- Measures:
  - bdd-stats on RR 84 (stats.sh) at rounds 4, 5, 6, 8, 15, 30 and 60. Every arm's classifiers equal the BDT's
    through round 3, so bddstudy's bdt-sN stats at rounds 3-8 are the exact no-split counterfactual.
  - Held-out losses (evalcal.sh) at rounds 20, 40 and 60, with the calibration fitted on the other fold (even / odd
    parts) and paired 95% t intervals over the 10 parts.
  - analyze.py writes analysis.txt; --verbose writes analysis-verbose.txt, which lists every R' node.

WHY, AND WHAT TO TRY NEXT
Both options inherit a problem from copies.
- A. Raw regret against a node's own children is not a skill measure. It is about zero for an R' over fresh copies
  and grows as any children diverge. Even the root's first split (0.098 -> 0.107) came two rounds after the leaf
  split that made its children, and a rise there can come from the children changing rather than from the router.
  - Next: measure an R''s regret against its base children (the routed leaf against the best of the shared
    children), so a new R' starts at the old router's level.
  - Or use a skill score, for example regret relative to always taking the router's most-used child.
- B. Leaf-loss targets tie exactly wherever the sub-routers agree.
  - Next: skip R''s update on positions where every sub-router reaches the same leaf, since they carry no information.
  - Or break ties by the sub-routers' routing errors.
  - Either would let the copies get traffic and diverge, as the leaves after a leaf split do: LR leaf losses never tie.
