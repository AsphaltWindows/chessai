routercmp: NB against LR error routers on the same fixed LR leaves (2026-11-08)
User: "can we do a similar comparison of Le and Ne routers for the same LR leaves? maybe there's a difference in how
well they predict the correct leaf to send the position to".

VERDICT: with the leaves held fixed, neither router kind is clearly better at sending positions to the right leaf.
- On each tree's own self-play positions, NB-routed and LR-routed trees differ by at most 0.0017 nats of calibrated
  log-loss (under 1%). The sign flips with the leaf set and the data size.
- No small-data advantage for NB holds across the settings.
- LR routers do pick the child that turned out best more often (6-17 points more at the root). They do it mostly by
  sending nearly everything to the child that is most often best. NB routers spread positions out, and lose the same
  or less when they pick wrong.

The one consistent difference is concentration. On the same leaves, LR routers put positions into fewer leaves than
NB routers in all 4 settings. Effective leaves at full data, NB against LR: 6.4 against 2.0, 6.2 against 4.3, 5.5
against 4.6, and 2.6 against 1.0.

The live league trees show the same thing, much more strongly:
- Every LR-router error tree without sharing (T-L-Le-c, -merge, -fresh, -pre) routes about 84% of its self-play to
  one leaf, 1.5-1.8 effective leaves. T-L-Le-c's champion v1505 sent 99.9% of cycle 30's self-play to a single leaf.
- The NB-router error trees run on 5.1-6.5 effective leaves.

So the league gap (Ne above Le by about 70 Elo in 4 of 5 pairs) is more likely the training feedback than routing
skill at any one moment:
- An LR router favours the leaf that is most often best.
- That leaf gets the data and gets better.
- The tree collapses towards one leaf.

Leaf sharing breaks this: T-L-Le-share3-c runs on 8.5 effective leaves, which fits share3 closing the gap. This test
does not prove the feedback. It shows that the static difference is too small to explain about 70 Elo, and that the
concentration difference is real.

Setup
- Binary: bin/chessai-routercmp, md5 8d607093. It is chessai-lrnb plus two commands (routercmp-src = lrnb-src plus
  routercmp.patch):
  - refit-routers TREE GAMES OUT [--fresh-router cnbp|lr]: loads a tree and makes every router an untrained one of
    the given kind, as a split makes it. It then trains only the routers, on train's positions in train's order (the
    same seed, so the same shuffle for both kinds).
    - It uses the tree code's own error routing: at each delegator on the path, every child's subtree predicts the
      position, and the router learns the child with the smallest raw log-loss (the Best target, first on ties).
      The router is trained before it routes the position on.
    - No leaf trains, splits, merges or resets. The LR rate is 0.05, the trees' own.
  - route-stats MODEL GAMES: for each delegator, over the positions that reach it:
    - how often the router picks the child whose subtree had the smallest realized raw log-loss;
    - the mean regret (the picked child's loss minus the best child's);
    - the shares routed to each child, and the shares each child is best for;
    - every leaf's loss on every position, and each position's leaf (--leaves-out).
- Identity checks (identity/):
  - eval-loss of both trees on the test is identical with chessai-routercmp, chessai-lrnb and chessai-cyc5.
  - Flat LR and BDT training is byte-identical to chessai-lrnb's.
  - All 11 leaves are byte-identical before and after a refit.
  - route-stats' tree loss equals eval-loss's raw log-loss (1.9616).
- Leaf sets: the champions T-L-Ne-c v1681 ("ne", leaves grown under NB routers) and T-L-Le-c v1505 ("le", leaves grown
  under LR routers). Both have 5 splits and 11 LR leaves (copies in trees/).
- Arms per leaf set:
  - fresh NB routers and fresh LR routers, each on nested subsets of about 10k, 30k, 100k, 300k and 1M positions,
    and on all of the training data;
  - the tree's own routers, as they are;
  - the best single leaf, chosen on one fold and scored on the other;
  - the per-position oracle over the leaves (raw; a loose bound, as lrnb noted).
- Two data sets:
  - c15, as the directive and lrnb: offline-c15, trained on RRs 80-83 (2.3M positions) and tested on RR 84 in 10
    parts. Calibration is held out as in lrnb (A = even parts, B = odd).
  - sp, each tree's own positions (selfplay.sh): the tree plays itself through arena --out at depth 0 with jitter10
    and --use-probs, as the lineages' self-play does.
    - 4 training rounds of 2250 games: 2.28M (ne) and 2.30M (le) positions.
    - 10 test parts of 230 games, each with its own seed: 579k and 585k positions.
    - 94-95% of the positions are from drawn games.
    - Subsets are prefixes of each round's outcome files, at lrnb's fractions.
- 95% intervals: mean of the 10 per-part paired differences, t with 9 df.

Reading
- Static routing skill does not separate the two kinds: the differences are tiny and change sign.
- What separates them is how much they concentrate. A plausible mechanism:
  - An LR router is a roughly calibrated estimate of P(best child | position). When one child is best most of the
    time, its argmax stays on that child unless the position's features strongly say otherwise.
  - NB's posteriors are overconfident (lrnb: a raw draw slope of about 0.1), so its argmax follows weaker evidence
    and spreads positions.
- In a live tree, spreading keeps several leaves trained. Concentrating feeds the favoured leaf until the tree is
  one LR.
- In the league, sharing lifts the LR-router tree that collapses (T-L-Le-share3-c 1796 against T-L-Le-c 1690) and
  not the NB one that spreads (T-L-Ne-share-c 1762 against T-L-Ne-c 1800). That fits, but these are single pairs.

Caveats
- The leaves are frozen. The feedback (routing shapes which leaf learns) is outside this test. The live lineages'
  routing shares are the evidence for it.
- One champion per kind. Their leaves differ a lot: le is effectively one good leaf on its own positions.
- Self-play positions are 94-95% from drawn games, so the absolute differences are small.
- The raw best-child target is noisy: one child always looks best on the realized outcome. This holds for both kinds
  and is what the tree code trains on.
