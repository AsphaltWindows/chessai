TAN routers in LR-leaf trees (2026-11-07). Verdict: they work, but they act like CNBP routers. They are not broken,
and nothing here says they would beat CNBP routers. In self-play every split merges back, as with CNBP routers.
Expect T-L-Tc-merge-c and T-L-Te-merge-c to track T-L-Nc-merge-c and T-L-Ne-merge-c, which mostly sit as flat LR.

Code: ../tanroute-src = ../nbmix-src + tanroute.patch.
- NodeKind::Tan / NodeModel::Tan: a TAN node of a delegation tree, with fixed m (--router tan --tan-m M). It can also
  be --leaf tan or --error-router tan.
- While training, the router predicts from its current counts with the tree of its last refresh
  (Tan::predict_counts). It relearns its tree at each refresh (each round), and has no parents until its first
  refresh with counts.
- Text model: the prediction tables. State file: every pair count, bit-exact.
- Options and files of existing kinds are unchanged. --tan-lambda/--tan-games are refused on tree nodes, and TAN
  nodes only in --model bdt.

Binaries:
- ../bin/chessai-tanroute2 (md5 a25836325c9571bdd1b88aa227f450e1): use this one.
- ../bin/chessai-tanroute (md5 7ff98967577d85b171a15add78a92e7c): the first build, superseded. Its fresh router used
  a star from feature 0 until its first refresh.

Checks:
- Unit tests: 128 pass. They cover predict_counts = predict after refresh, text and state round trips, tan nodes
  as leaf and router, and a TAN cluster router separating two groups without labels.
  - With small m (5 against about 500 per child), one child's TAN takes part of the other group (406/75 against
    0/519). With m near the counts it splits cleanly.
- Identity: chessai-tanroute2 = chessai-nbmix2 on every non-log output of ../probit/identity/run2.sh (0 diff
  lines; out2-tanroute2, out2-tanroute). Every existing kind is the same.

Caveat: offline, a router made mid-round predicts as CNBP until that round ends (up to about 600k examples), so
these partitions start as CNBP's. In the league the hash warm-up (1M examples) spans about 3 refreshes, so the
router routes as a TAN from the start.

Self-play smoke (smoke/run.sh). The league recipes scaled down: 500 games a round, --split-threshold 150000, 20
rounds, seed 21, depth 0, 2 threads. Each TAN run equals its CNBP comparator until the first split.

| run | merges | revisions | revision gains per example |
|---|---|---|---|
| tc | 5 | 5 | −0.0031 to −0.00075 |
| nc | 5 | 5 | −0.0030 to −0.0004 |
| te | 4 | 4 | −0.0009 to −0.0004 |
| ne | 5 | 5 | −0.0013 to −0.0004 |

- Every split loses to the unsplit shadow at its revision and merges back, as in the from-scratch league runs (T-L-Nc-merge-s21:
  7 merges in rounds 132-559).
- CPU, user-s for 20 rounds: tc 33.1 against nc 29.8 (11% more), te 31.6 against ne 27.7 (14% more).
  - The extra is training: about 0.5-0.8 s more in a round with a split (the router's pair counts and its round-end
    Chow-Liu).
  - Play time per ply is the same within noise (about 4 CPU-us).
- State: about 8.7 MB per TAN router while split (te's last state). Only the last two states are kept. Model files
  are about the same size (630 KB).
