DRIFT MEASUREMENT, 2026-11-10: does mixed-architecture local training make the stream outdate a fixed model
faster, and so make gradual forgetting more valuable? (models/from-scratch/drift/)

The user's hypothesis: "local training with different architectures produces larger diversity of games, which
makes gradual forgetting more valuable." Context: in cluster-error (14 lines: LR and probit leaves, cluster and
error routers), flat probit F-P went from 25-33 Elo below flat LR F-L to 24 above it by cycles 550-571. LR
plateaued by ~cycle 200 in single-architecture experiments, while probit was still climbing at 1000. Probit
forgets (tau 1e-5 adds variance per use). LR is AdaGrad and has no explicit forgetting.

ANSWER IN SHORT
- Cluster-error's stream does not outdate a fixed model faster than the single-architecture streams.
  - After cycle ~200-300, no stream measurably outdates a frozen flat model within 50-100 cycles. In
    cluster-error that is up to 882k games and 87M positions later.
  - Early (t=100), every stream except split-growth does, and single-split-probit does it fastest.
  - Per game, cluster-error outdates more slowly than single-split and single-split-probit. Per cycle it is faster
    only for F-L at k=10-50 from t=100: 0.003-0.005 against ~0.
- Step sizes: LR's AdaGrad multiplier keeps falling as ~1/sqrt(positions seen), on the same curve in both
  experiments. Probit's variance sits at the tau equilibrium from version 25 on. The ratio grows smoothly, with
  nothing special at the 530-570 crossover.
- First half of the hypothesis: weakly yes. Cluster-error's position mix differs between nearby cycles 2-4 times
  as much as the single-architecture streams', but it is jitter, not drift.
- Second half: not supported. A frozen cluster-error F-P or F-L predicts cycles 501-550 (the crossover) as well as
  the current version. Both models forget within ~0.3-1.5 cycles, far faster than any drift. Offline, F-P has been
  ahead of F-L in winner loss by about the same margin since cycle 101.

METHOD
- The experiments delete their training games, so I regenerated the local games of chosen cycles exactly. I ran
  replay-games (chessai-hybrid7) with each experiment's players, their own-calibration games (regenerated with the
  config's calibration settings) and its seed. All 86 samples match tour/games.csv game for game (results and
  plies).
- Samples are of the local set (games with at least one current version):
  - cluster-error: 1 round per cycle, 992 games, ~97k positions;
  - single-split and single-split-probit: 3 of 5 rounds, 918 games, ~92-108k positions;
  - split-growth: 5 of 9 rounds, 910 games, ~98-113k positions.

  Cycles sampled are t+1, t+2, t+5, t+10, t+20, t+50 and t+100, for t = 100, 300 and 500. Split-growth only ran
  to cycle 400, so it uses t = 100, 200 and 300.
- Probes:
  - cluster-error: F-L and F-P;
  - single-split: F-L;
  - single-split-probit: F-P;
  - split-growth: F-L.

  Version v has trained on cycles 1..v and plays cycle v+1. Each sample is scored with eval-loss --use-probs (the
  model's own probabilities) twice: with the frozen version t, and with the version that played the cycle,
  t+k-1.
- Losses, per position, natural log:
  - plain = 3-class log-loss over all positions;
  - winner = -log of the win/loss-renormalized probability of the actual winner, decisive games only.

  Winner loss is the staleness measure. Plain loss is dominated by draw calibration, which swings from version to
  version (see below). SEs are clustered by game.
- delta(k) = loss of version t on cycle t+k minus its loss on cycle t+1, as asked. It compares different games,
  and its SE (0.02 for winner loss, 0.06-0.07 for plain) exceeds every effect found. The main measure is therefore
  EXCESS(k): frozen version t minus the then-current version, on the same games, paired by game. EXCESS(1) is 0 by
  definition.

VERDICT
- Does cluster-error's stream outdate a fixed model faster than the single-architecture streams?
  - Per cycle: no. From t=300 nothing outdates anywhere. At t=100:
    - cluster-error's F-P outdates more slowly than single-split-probit's;
    - its F-L outdates earlier than single-split's (0.003-0.005 from k=10, against ~0 to k=50), but less by k=100
      (0.003 against 0.012).
  - Per game: no, and by a wider margin, since it plays 3.6x the games per cycle.
  - At matched positions seen it is ~1 SE more stale than single-split and single-split-probit, which is not
    significant.
- Step sizes: LR keeps falling (x0.22 from v25 to v575 in cluster-error, 1/sqrt of positions) while probit is
  level from v25. The ratio grows smoothly through the crossover.
- First half (different architectures make the games more diverse): weakly supported, as cycle-to-cycle jitter of
  the position mix (2-4x between nearby cycles) with shorter games. It is not drift: there is no accumulation with
  lag after cycle 100, the decisive share is binomial, and length and phase mix are flat. Over 50-100 cycles the
  single-architecture streams drift about as far.
- Second half (which makes gradual forgetting more valuable): not supported.
  - There is no drift for forgetting to track in cluster-error after cycle ~200: frozen F-L@500 and F-P@500 match
    the current versions through cycle 550.
  - Both models already forget within ~0.3 (F-P) to ~1.5 (F-L) cycles.
  - Jitter that does not persist from one cycle to the next (JS at k=2 equals JS at k=100) favours a longer
    memory, not a shorter one.
  - The F-P/F-L crossover has no counterpart in offline winner loss, so whatever moved Elo at 530-570, these data
    don't show it to be drift-tracking.
