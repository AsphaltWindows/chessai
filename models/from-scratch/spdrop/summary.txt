Topic spdrop (2026-11-07/08 night, the coordinator's diagnostic for a design question): after one cycle of self-play
(about 60 CPU s), several lineages' new versions are 140-220 below their champion in the new league (cycles/champ),
and the own step on league games wins it back in its gate (0.63-0.81). Own-step versions never play in the
tournament.

Verdict (all three questions answered; about 12 CPU-min):
1. The drop is real, at ply 2 only, and kind-specific. Over cycles 2-4 at ply 2: FM -147, T-L-Le* -105, MoE -91,
   T-L-LLs* -70, T-N-Le -60, flat LR -21; the rest (CNBP-routed trees, NB-leaf trees, TAN, D-L-Nc) within about
   30, except T-N-LLs at 42. At ply 1 the same versions hold or gain
   (FM 71, T-L-Le 44). The first run's history shows the same, pooled over 132 switches between self-play and own steps.
   Within each lineage it is steady from cycle to cycle, not noise. It is not bigger for d2jit; T-L-NLs-d2jit-c's
   drop is a different case (version noise, a lucky old-peak champion, a lower slope).
2. Depth-0 self-play moves the model's skill from ply-2 positions to ply-1 positions, all of it in the first round
   of 1000 games. The own step, half of whose games are the ply-2 entry's, moves it back.
   - The kinds that swing most have learned local parts: FM factors, MoE gates, LR routers over LR leaves. Flat LR
     barely swings. LR routers over NB leaves mostly don't swing (T-N-Lc 12, T-N-LLs 42); T-N-Le (-60) does.
   - It is forgetting ply-2-relevant positions, not draw drift (universal, and the policy ignores draws) and not the
     cal-own slope (wrong direction, except T-L-NLs-d2jit-c).
3. The own step wins the whole drop back: own-step versions are level with their champions (mean 5, se 15).
   - The champions are own-step versions, so the champion test compares the bottom of each lineage's swing with a
     top.
   - (a) Rating the own-step version through its gate: right on average (152 against the panel's 134), off by up to
     100 for one lineage, and the gate plays the wrong policy (WldScoreJitter10, not the league's).
   - (b) The own step before the tournament, on last cycle's games: it recovers as much as this cycle's games
     (O'5 - E5 119 against O3 - E3 124, three lineages). The entering version would be level with its champion
     (mean -5). This is the fix that works.
   - (c) A smaller self-play rate: not cheap. One round already makes the whole shift, and a quarter of a cycle's
     step in weight space costs 85.

Questions:
1. Is the drop real once pooled over cycles and versions? Which kinds?
2. What does self-play change: held-out loss, draw prediction, the cal-own slope?
3. What would the cheap fixes do (rate the own-step version through its gate; own step before the tournament on last
   cycle's games; a smaller self-play rate)? Estimated offline or in arenas only.

== 2. What self-play changes

Own-game calibration (q2cal.py -> q2cal.out; from tournament.log and the gates' lines in cycles/runs/<lineage>.log):
- Every lineage, drop or not, flips its draw calibration with each step.
  - Champions and own-step versions (O): offsets -1.5 to -2.9, slope_decisive 0.5-0.7. On their own games they
    predict too few draws.
  - Post-self-play versions (E): offsets 1 to 4.9, slope_decisive 0.8-1.7. They predict far too many draws.
  - Self-play games are 85-93% draws (results.txt), own games 55-68%, league games 3% at ply 2 and 15-19% at ply 1
    (ratings.csv).
  - So the draw drift is universal, and by itself it isn't the drop, because the controls flip just as much (T-L-Ne-c,
    F-L-d0soft-c, T-L-NNs-c, T-L-Ne-merge-c).
- The league's policy (log-odds softmax, ln P(win) - ln P(loss)) ignores the draw probability. The offsets cancel
  between moves, so only slope_winner (sw) moves play.
- sw, E against C/O:
  - FM-c 0.93-1.36 against 0.72-0.74;
  - T-L-Le-fresh-c 1.53-1.69 against 1.21-1.42;
  - T-L-Le-c 1.52-1.79 against 1.23-1.37;
  - T-L-NLs-d2jit-c 0.60-0.85 against 0.76-1.02.
  - The controls barely move (T-L-Ne-c 1.59-1.69 against 1.55-1.76).
  - A larger sw is greedier play, which leaguecal found helps (about 130 per unit ln slope). So it doesn't explain the
    ply-2 drop, except perhaps T-L-NLs-d2jit-c's: ln(0.65/0.93) is about -0.36, or about -45.

The gate doesn't play the league's policy:
- cycle.py's gate_match calls `arena` without policy flags, and arena's default policy is WldScoreJitter10: expected
  score with jitter, the self-play policy. The tournament's default is log-odds softmax at T 0.1. The arena log shows
  it: "(WldScoreJitter10, depth 2)".
- The gate's own games and calibration do follow the league's recipe. So every gate in both runs measured the own step
  in the self-play policy at depth 2, not in the league's.
- MoE-c, cycle 3 (own.sh, then arenas by hand, both deterministic):
  - The regenerated own games of v389 and v390 give the logged calibrations exactly. The gate replays exactly:
    +73 -25 =2, 182.
  - In the league's policy over 200 games, v390 (O3) against v389 (E3) is +129 -61 =10, 123 (75 to 176).
  - The own step's gain is real head to head, and somewhat smaller in the league's policy.

Held-out scores (evalrun.sh -> evals.out, evaltab.py -> evals-table.out; 32 CPU s):
- The positions come from cycle 4's league games, copied out of the live pool while the own steps ran (grab.py).
  - The league sample is 2% of all pairings, references out; ply 2 (both sides at ply 2) is 1420 games and 125k
    positions, ply 1 is 385 games and 43k positions.
  - The other two sets are the lineage's ply-2 entry's games and E3's own games.
  - The pool copy also let ownstep.py replay MoE-c's cycle-4 own step byte for byte (md5 942c3d40... = moe398.model).
- Fitted winner log-loss is the log-loss of who won, among decisive positions, with a calibration fitted on the set.
  So it measures the log-odds that the league's policy reads; lower is better. "Raw" is the uncalibrated full
  log-loss.

- Depth-0 self-play moves the model's skill from ply-2 positions to ply-1 positions, and the own step moves it back.
  E minus O3, on ply-2 positions and on ply-1 positions:
  - FM-c: 0.041 and -0.013;
  - T-L-Le-fresh-c: 0.026 and -0.035;
  - MoE-c: 0.019 and -0.018 (E4: 0.012 and -0.013);
  - F-L-d0soft-c: 0.009 and -0.005.
- The sizes rank like the Elo drops: FM and T-L-Le large, MoE next, flat LR small. That matches the league data: the
  ply-2 entries lose and the ply-1 entries hold or gain.
- Self-play's own games (85-93% draws) and 1-ply play are a different distribution from ply-2 league play (97%
  decisive). The own step's games are half the ply-2 entry's.
- Models with learned local capacity track whichever distribution they were trained on last: FM's factors, MoE's gate
  and experts, LR routers' partitions over LR leaves. Flat LR and count-routed trees (CNBP routers) move much less.
  - NB-leaf trees under LR routers mostly don't swing: T-N-Lc 12 and T-N-LLs 42, but T-N-Le -60. So it isn't the
    LR router alone.
- The same direction as depth-train's equal-data result (IDEAS.md, look-ahead training by classifier): LR trained on
  ply-2 league games against ply-1 games was 201 at ply 2 and -34 at ply 1.
  - Not shown directly here, but topic 4d found LR routers re-route up to a third of positions per version.
- Draws: every post-self-play version, the control's too, has raw log-loss 2.3-2.8 on ply-2 league positions (O
  and C: 0.64-0.68). Self-play's 90% draws make the raw model predict draws in a 97%-decisive league. The cal-own
  calibration repairs the draw level (positive offsets), and the league's policy ignores it anyway. So the draw drift is
  a symptom of the same shift, not the cause of the drop.
- T-L-NLs-d2jit-c is the exception, and a different case:
  - Its self-play is at depth 2, with about 25% draws (results.txt). So its E versions don't over-predict draws (raw
    0.71) and don't shift between plies.
  - Its E3 (v1713) is worse on every set, a bad single version. Its E4 (v1728) is level with C on all of them.
  - Its champion is an old peak (a lucky version). Its E versions also calibrate to a lower sw (0.60-0.85 against
    0.76-1.02), which is hotter play, worth about -40.
  - So its drop is version noise plus peak selection plus the slope, not the ply shift.

== 3. Fixes

(a) Rating the own-step version through its gate: first-run evidence (q3gate.py -> q3gate.out, no CPU).
- While a lineage was dormant, each kept own step's version entered the field after the version it started from.
  So the final fit rates both: 348 such pairs.
- The gates (1000 games at depth 2, WldScoreJitter10): mean 15 (se 0.7), after selection (kept only at 50% or more).
- The league, the same pairs: 10 (se 1.8) at ply 2 and 0.0 (se 1.2) at ply 1.
- The slope of the league's ply-2 difference on the gate's Elo is 0.47 (correlation 0.19). The gate overstates the
  league gain by about half, from the keep rule's selection plus the policy mismatch.
- Own steps add strength at ply 2 only, even in a chain of own steps with no self-play between.

(a), continued. Panel arenas in the league's policy (panel.sh -> panel.out, panel.py -> panel-fit.out):
- Each version plays 5 fixed champions of other lineages (F-L-c v2860, T-L-NNs-merge-c v1070, F-L-d0soft-c v2367,
  F-L-d1play-c v3595, F-N-c v1756), 60 games each, ply 2, log-odds softmax T 0.1, cal-own, the same seeds for every
  version.
- Each version is rated by maximum likelihood against the panel's ratings in the league's latest fit (cycle 4's).
  Standard errors are about 21 per version and 30 per difference.
- The panel reproduces the league: C and E3 come within 0-25 of their league ratings, and E3 - C within 8-20.

| lineage | E3 - C, panel (league) | O3 - E3, panel | gate (WldScoreJitter10, 100 games) | head to head, league policy (200 games) | O3 - C, panel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MoE-c (v373, v389, v390) | -105 (-118) | 82 | 182 | 123 | -23 |
| FM-c (v669, v682, v683) | -135 (-122) | 162 | 151 | 164 | 27 |
| T-L-Le-fresh-c (v742, v758, v759) | -132 (-112) | 129 | 92 | 76 | -3 |
| T-L-NLs-d2jit-c (v1462, v1713, v1714) | -139 (-147) | 161 | 182 | 228 | 21 |
| mean | -128 | 134 (se 15) | 152 | 148 | 5 (se 15) |

- The own step wins back the whole drop. Each own-step version O3 is level with its lineage's champion in the
  league's terms (-23 to 27).
- These four lineages oscillate about their champions' level: self-play takes them about 130 down at ply 2 and the own
  step brings them back. They made no net progress at ply 2 over cycles 2-3.
- The champions themselves are own-step versions (the first run's dormant chains), so the champion test compares the
  bottom of each oscillation with a top.
- Rating O3 as E3's league rating plus its gate's Elo would be right on average here (152 against 134). It would
  have been wrong by up to 100 for a single lineage: MoE-c would have scored 1747, a false new champion 63 above C,
  where the panel puts it at 1662.
- A gate of 100 games has a standard error of about 40 Elo, 3 times the fit's 14. The first run's gates overstated
  their league gains by about 2 (slope 0.47).

(c) A smaller self-play rate. T-L-Le-fresh-c's cycle-4 self-play, O3 (v759) to E4 (v767), 8 rounds of 1000 games.
- The real trajectory (traj.sh -> traj.out):
  - The fitted winner log-loss on league ply-2 positions goes from 0.4262 (v759) to 0.4502 after one round (v760),
    then stays at 0.448-0.454 through v767.
  - On ply-1 positions it goes from 0.3425 to 0.3073 at once, then stays at 0.307-0.314.
  - In the panel: v759 1695, v760 (one round) 1555, v761 (two rounds) 1543, v767 1602 (league 1564).
  - So the whole shift happens in the first round. One round of self-play (1000 games) undoes the own step, and one
    own step (3650 league games, 285k positions) undoes 8 rounds.
- Weight interpolation as a proxy for a smaller rate (interp.py, interprun.sh -> interp.out):
  - interp/le-aA.model = O3 + A (E4 - O3) in every LR node (leaves and routers).
  - Ply-2 loss: 0.4262, 0.4306, 0.4433, 0.4492, 0.4504 at A = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1. Ply 1: 0.3425, 0.3244,
    0.3079, 0.3056, 0.3069.
  - In the panel: 1695 at A = 0, 1610 at 0.25, 1538 at 0.5, and E4 1602.
  - A quarter of the cycle's step already costs 85 at ply 2. The midpoint is worse than either end, probably because
    mixed LR routers send positions to leaves fitted under the other routing.
- Estimate: a smaller self-play rate does not remove the drop unless it is cut far below one round's worth, which
  would stall self-play's learning too. Not a cheap fix.
- Caveat: interpolation is only a first-order proxy for a smaller rate. The trajectory is the stronger evidence.

(b) The own step before the tournament, on last cycle's games (bfix.sh -> bfix.out, bfix.py -> bfix-fit.out):
- O'5 = own(E5; cycle 4's games of E4's entries).
  - It is trained by ownstep.py from the pool copy, exactly as the driver trains its own step (byte-identical
    replays of cycle 4's O4 for MoE-c, FM-c and T-L-Le-fresh-c).
  - E5 is cycle 5's post-self-play version. O'5 and E5 play the same panel as above.

| lineage | E5 (panel) | O'5 (panel) | O'5 - E5 (se 30) | cycle 3's O3 - E3 (this cycle's games) | O'5 - C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MoE-c | v405 1558 | 1651 | 93 | 82 | -33 |
| FM-c | v694 1429 | 1588 | 159 | 162 | 5 |
| T-L-Le-fresh-c | v776 1605 | 1711 | 106 | 129 | 13 |
| mean | | | 119 | 124 | -5 |

- Games one cycle old work as well as this cycle's games. The step learns ply-2 league positions, not this
  version's own games.
- The order would then be: self-play, the own step on last cycle's games and its gate (against the post-self-play
  version), then the field gets the kept version and the tournament. It costs nothing extra.
  - The league would rate versions in the same phase as the champions, and new champions would become possible.
  - The pool of the previous cycle would have to be kept until the next cycle's own step (keep_pools), about 1 GB of
    games for the 11 lineages copied here and more for the whole field.
- The gate's comparison (O' against E) is unchanged, and so is its policy issue.

Gate policy: cycle.py's gate_match should pass --a-policy log-odds-softmax --b-policy log-odds-softmax
--temperature 0.1, as the tournament plays (a driver change; not made).
- MoE-c's cycle-3 gate replays exactly in the default policy: 182. In the league's policy over 200 games it is 123,
  and in the panel 82.
- With 100 games a gate's standard error is about 40.

== Files and CPU

- q1.py, q1b.py, q2cal.py, q3gate.py: records only, no games (q1.out, q1b.out, q2cal.out, q3gate.out).
- grab.py: the cycle-4 pool copy (pool4/, 1.0 GB: every pairing of 11 lineages plus a 2% sample).
- merge.py: the scoring sets (sets/, 0.5 GB).
- ownstep.py: replays the driver's own step.
- own.sh: own games by the league's recipe (owncal/). The regenerated ones match the driver's logs exactly.
- panel.sh/panel.py: 371 CPU s. bfix.sh/bfix.py: 147. interprun.sh: 61. traj.sh: 44. evalrun.sh: 35. The
  gate replay and head to head: 38. Identity replays and checks: about 25.
- In all, about 730 CPU s (12 CPU-min), at nice 19 with 2 threads.
- Everything is under models/from-scratch/spdrop (2.0 GB). The live dirs were only read: state.json, cycles.csv,
  logs, model files, the pool (copied while the own steps ran), and owncal (copied).
