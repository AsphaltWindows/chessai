Topic 3 (2026-11-07/08 night): blend in self-play. Does soft-routed self-play make better training data than hard
routing?

Answer: no. No blend lineage is proposed.
- Per round of data from scratch, blended self-play does make better trees at ply 2: 69 (45 to 93) over five version
  pairs.
- But it costs 2.2-2.3x the CPU, and at equal CPU the hard arm is ahead: -35 at ply 1, -73 at ply 2.
- Resumed from a mature lineage (the league's case), blended data is slightly worse at ply 1 (-20) and level at
  ply 2.
- Single versions vary by up to 75 Elo at ply 1 and 200 at ply 2 between adjacent versions. Only the pooled
  comparisons below are reliable; single pairs misled at first.

Pooled comparisons (the blend-trained arm's Elo, 95% interval; cal-own specs, league policy; each version pair 400
games at ply 1 and 120 at ply 2, seed = version; pool.py run4.out run5.out):

| comparison | pairs | ply 1 (2000 games) | ply 2 (600 games) |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) resumed, equal data: a-blend - a-hard | v337-v341 | -20 (-32 to -8) | -13 (-40 to 14) |
| (b) from scratch, equal data: b-blend - b-hard | v20-v24 | 10 (-1 to 21) | 69 (45 to 93) |
| (b) from scratch, equal CPU: b-blend v20-v24 - b-hard | v31, v34, v37, v39, v43 | -35 (-47 to -24) | -73 (-98 to -47) |

- Equal CPU: each b-hard version is the one whose cumulative self-play CPU matches the b-blend version's. Play runs

- In each window both arms have the same tree structure.

Version noise:
- Against the same opponent, b-blend v24, adjacent b-hard versions differ widely:
  - v42: -89 at ply 1, -238 at ply 2;
  - v43: -15 and -38 (run5, 400 and 120 games);
  - v39: -106 and -219 (paired with v23).
- b-hard's tree has had the same 11 leaves (the split limit) since v18, so this is not split timing.
- Its own-game calibrations differ too. The fitted parameters for v42 are 1.21, 0.67, 1.02, 0.79; for v43 0.97, 0.69,
  1.07, 1.05.
- run6.sh plays v42 against v43 directly, with cal-own and with the same held-out calibration for both, to tell the
  trees from their calibrations. Results below when it finishes.
- For the league: single-version ratings at ply 2 may carry noise of this size, and a peak rule picks the lucky
  versions.

Why: an earlier hypothesis, that blended play hides the seams from self-play so the leaves stop being corrected and
search suffers, rested on the single-pair -104 at ply 2. The pooled results don't support it: from scratch, the blend
arm is better at ply 2. What the evidence does support:
- The blend arms end with their leaves further apart: (a) 0.667 against 0.512; (b) 0.605 against 0.517.
- Per round from scratch, blended data trains better ply-2 players.
- Per CPU it loses, and from a mature tree it doesn't help.

What a blend lineage would have needed (for the record):
- Training: --play-blend 1 on chessai-blendplay. No driver change.
- League entries: a per-lineage play spec, blend:1:OWN:bdt:PATH. That is a driver change.
- Tournament binary: unchanged, since chessai-sparsefit loads blend specs.
- Cost: 2.2-2.3x self-play CPU, 4-5x a league game.

CPU spent:
- Self-play: 712.6 CPU-s (a-hard 83.6, a-blend 193.5, b-blend 162.9, b-hard 272.6).
- Own games and arenas: 3.5 wall-min at 2 threads, so at most 7 CPU-min.
- About 19 CPU-min in all, plus about 8 for run2-run6.

Code: ../blendplay-src = ../eloss-src + blendplay.patch.
- New self-play option --play-blend TAU, for --model bdt only.
- The players choose their moves with copies of the tree whose predictions blend the leaves' log-probabilities.
  - The weights are the routers' probabilities softened by TAU: Graph::leaf_weights, paths below 0.001 dropped.
  - This is blend:TAU:... without recalibrating the leaves, since there are no own games inside self-play.
- Training and saved models are unchanged. It goes through the same play-copy hook as --probit-play-eps.
- Train accepts and ignores it.
- New unit test: one leaf blends to itself; at tau 0 two leaves average; without blending, the routed leaf. 139 tests
  pass.

Binary: ../bin/chessai-blendplay (md5 ee5167b428b9e36481aaa21847a28724).
- Without --play-blend it is identical to chessai-eloss: 0 non-log diff lines on all four identity scripts
  (identity.sh -> identity.out).
- It was built before a one-line fix to the new test's setup (params(true) -> params(false), test code only). A
  rebuild after the fix has md5 0628ed8c6b4f04318bbeb8f4dda7f198 and was not installed. The release code is the same.

Tree shapes on the LR d2 held-out set (leaf-pair mean |log-odds difference|):
- a-hard v341: 7 leaves, 0.512. a-blend v341: 7 leaves, 0.667.
- b-hard v24: 11 leaves, 0.517. b-blend v24: 11 leaves, 0.605.

Self-play's move choice is mostly jitter.
- WldScoreJitter10 multiplies each move's expected score by U(0.9, 1.1), and plays a non-top move 84-86% of the time
  for LR models (nbcal).
- So blending, which reorders close moves, changes self-play's move distribution little. That blended play still
  changes the data this much says the seams matter.
