  - Source: models/from-scratch/fastbin/hybrid11-src, a copy of hybrid10-src. Only src/fixed.rs and src/search.rs
    changed. Same commands, flags and files. Built offline at nice 19.
  - Changed (the module doc says it too). These apply only to the values-only fixed-point search (OUTCOME = false in
    negamax_fx and child_fx: flat LR, probit and NB under root_values, which tournaments, arenas, replay-games and the
    log-odds policies use):
    1. Acc::move_sums (fixed.rs) gives a child's integer sums from the move itself. It looks at the squares the move
       changes, the side to move, the en passant file, and the castling rights, which it clones and recomputes only
       when a king or rook moves or a rook is captured. Acc::child_sums played the move and compared the two
       encodings instead.
    2. The ordering keys at depth >= 2 come from move_sums: no position copy and play per key, and one key buffer per
       depth, reused. The first move searched is the first of the largest keys, which is what hybrid10's stable sort
       put first. The rest are sorted only if that move doesn't cut off.
    3. Leaves (depth 0, log-odds values) take their sums from the move. A leaf reached by an irreversible move gets no
       hash, because it can't repeat a position. A reversible leaf's repetition is checked against the stack without
       pushing.
    4. A reversible move's hash comes from the move: side to move and the piece's two squares. Before, shakmaty's
       update_zobrist_hash declined after any double pawn push and for every king move, and the search re-hashed the
       whole position.
  - Unchanged: the outcome search (root_outcomes, expected_outcome: self-play's wld-score policies and search labels),
    and the compound (trees and DAGs at plies 1-2), fastprobit (F-P at ply 2) and float negamax (trees past ply 2,
    FM) paths. CHESSAI_MOVESUMS=off gives hybrid10's code everywhere, for checks.

METHOD (why nothing can change)
- The sums are i64. move_sums adds the same feature changes as child_sums, in another order, and integer addition
  doesn't depend on order, so the sums are identical. A unit test checks this on every legal move of 300 random games
  (over 1M moves, including castles, en passant, promotions and rook captures). CHESSAI_FX_CHECK=1 asserts it on every
  call at run time.
- The same sums give the same f64 keys. Taking the first index of the maximum under total_cmp, then stably sorting
  the rest, is exactly the stable sort's order. So the order is the same, the nodes are the same and the values are
  the same.
- An irreversible move resets the repetition window, so its child can't be a repetition. A reversible child's hash
  from the move equals the full hash; a unit test checks over 500k moves, and shakmaty's update agrees wherever it
  answers.
- So every root value is bit-identical, and the search visits the same nodes (the unit test asserts equal node
  counts). The games can't differ.

PROFILE NOTES (ply 4, F-L against itself; from the instrumented hybrid10 copy, hybrid11-prof, and eu-stack sampling)
- The search tree is near-minimal:
  - 87% of depth-1 cut nodes cut on their first move, 78% at depth 2;
  - 9.2M leaves in 489 plies.
  So the lever is the cost a node, not fewer nodes.
- Ruled out by measurement:
  - A transposition table: ~10.5% of leaves repeat, ~4% net after its own cost.
  - Killer moves first: the killer is the cut move in only 45% of cut nodes, so it's a net loss.
- What each part costs, measured by doing it twice (hybrid10, L, ply 4):
  - keys +1.0 s of ~4.3;
  - move generation +0.4;
  - leaf sums +0.36;
  - terminal checks +0.3;
  - playing moves +0.2.
  The sort, captures-first, Acc::child and the interior checks were within noise.
