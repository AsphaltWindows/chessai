WHAT CHANGED (every float operation in the same order; no reassociation, no FMA, no fast-math)
1. TAN pair counts (tan.rs, Tan::add). With one-hot labels at weight 1 and integer counts (always so here):
   - the parent pairs, the only ones predict_counts reads, are added at once, in the same cells, as before;
   - every other pair is counted in an integer batch over the nonzero values only. A pair's zero-value cells follow
     from each feature's value counts. The batch is added (one `+= k`) before anything else reads the counts:
     refresh (Chow-Liu, tables), forget, state and model writes.
   - Exact: every count is an integer below 2^52 (checked; past it, or with any other label, it falls back to the
     old loop), so k additions of 1.0 equal one addition of k, bit for bit.
2. TAN prediction while training (Tan::predict_counts_cached, used by delegation.rs node_output). A training step
   predicts the same position several times, and only one class's counts change in between. A class's log product
   depends on its own counts only, so it is reused while they are unchanged; the class prior is recomputed. The
   logs are added in the same order as before.
3. LR training at tree nodes (delegation.rs). A leaf or LR router that has just predicted the position in this step
   (the cache is cleared by any training) takes its gradient step from that prediction instead of predicting again.
4. Logreg::step with AVX2 (logreg.rs): four features at a time, each with the same operations as the scalar loop.
   Add, multiply, divide and sqrt are correctly rounded per lane; a blend reproduces "skip if the gradient is 0".
   Logreg::predict_log for 3 classes keeps three sums side by side, each in its old order.
5. parse_position (games.rs): a fast path for plain lines of 70 numbers. Anything else goes to the old parser.

CAVEATS
- NB routers and probit leaves gain least (0.8-0.94): CNBP training and Probit::update are untouched.
- The batch applies to one-hot labels at weight 1 with integer counts below 2^52, as in every live recipe. Soft
  labels, game-weighted TAN (--tan-games) and relative m (--tan-lambda) fall back to the old code: exact, not faster.
- On E-cores the AVX2 step gains little; the scheduler decides where a training job runs.
- Self-play training (selfplay-depth and other self-play lines) still runs the old binaries.
- No new unit tests: the identity runs are the test. They cover the live recipes (LR, NB and TAN routers; LR and
  probit leaves; error and cluster routes; splits and merges). Mixtures, FM, DAGs and Hoeffding trees share the
  changed TAN counting, LR step and parser, exact by the same arguments, but were not replayed.
- CPU used: about 40 CPU min at nice 19 (builds, profiles, A/B timing, the identity suite of ~15 CPU min, the
  split checks, arenas).
