lrnb: is LR strictly better than NB, or better in most circumstances but worse in some? (2026-11-08)
User: "whether LR are strictly better than NB models or LR are better in more circumstances than NB models are but
are worse in some also".

VERDICT: LR is better everywhere we looked. No group of positions has NB ahead, and no data size from 10k
positions up has NB ahead. NB only ties LR in a few places: at 10k training positions (on log-loss), and on the
1.8% of positions where NB is at least 97% confident. A gate that mixes the two per position gains nothing on
held-out data. So NB is not a specialist for some kind of position. It is a weaker model of the same thing.

Setup
- Benchmark: offline-c15. Training is cycle 15's round-robins 80-83 (2,308,895 positions). Test is round 84
  (575,099 positions in 10 parts).
- Models: LR uses F-L-c's recipe (--model lr --use-probs) and NB uses F-N-c's (--model cnbp --use-probs).
- Binary: bin/chessai-lrnb, md5 ff4aee20. It is chessai-sparsefit plus `eval-loss --dump FILE`, which writes the
  class, the 3 log-probabilities and the 70 features of each position (patch in lrnb-src/lrnb.patch). Its
  eval-loss output and its trained models are byte-identical to sparsefit's: lr-all = offline-c15 lr and
  nb-all = offline-c15 fn.
- Every loss is held out twice. The models never saw round 84. Each model's own 4-parameter calibration is
  fitted on one fold of the test parts (A = the even parts, B = the odd ones) and scored on the other fold.
  - A first try split the parts as p0-p4 against p5-p9. Its offsets differed by about 0.3 between the folds,
    because the parts are consecutive chunks of the outcome files, so they hold different pairings.
  - With the interleaved folds, lr-all scores 0.6234 against 0.6220 calibrated in-sample.
- 95% intervals come from the 10 parts (t, 9 df). Group means are pooled, and the variance is the ratio
  estimator's with the parts as clusters, so positions of one game stay in one cluster.

   The subsets are nested random subsets of pair-dirs: each round is shuffled with its own seed and then
   truncated. LR and NB are trained on the same stream.

   There is no crossover. At about 10k positions the two tie on log-loss, and NB is still worse on the winner
   loss. From 30k positions on, NB is significantly worse. NB flattens at about 0.74 from 300k positions while LR
   keeps improving, so the gap grows with data. If NB wins at all, it is below about 10k positions (a few dozen
   games). That is far below any league lineage.

2. Groups at full data (groups.out; NB-LR per group, with the league's current F-N-c - F-L-c in the last column)
   The offline pair is nb-all - lr-all. The league pair is F-N-c v2620 - F-L-c v4167, copied to m/league/ and
   calibrated the same way.

   There are 58 label-free bins. In 55 of them NB is worse with the interval above zero. The other three are
   ties:
   - NB confidence 0.97 and up: 10,189 positions (1.8%), 0.0072 [-0.0083, 0.0227].
   - F-N-c's confidence 0.97 and up: 4,482 positions, -0.0134 [-0.0885, 0.0618].
   - Ply unknown (the starts of part chunks): 1,326 positions, -0.0225 [-0.19, 0.15].

   The league pair never has F-N-c significantly ahead either: it is worse in 53 bins and ties in 5. The ties
   are F-N-c's confidence 0.97 and up, ply unknown, NB or F-N-c confidence under 0.40, and rarest entry
   100-999.

   - Overall: 0.1104 [0.079, 0.142]; league pair 0.1145 [0.067, 0.162].
   - Smallest gaps:
     - Where NB is confident: 0.90-0.97 gives 0.033 [0.016, 0.051], and 0.97 and up ties.
     - The opening: ply 1-10 gives 0.070 [0.045, 0.094], and all four castling rights give 0.076 [0.056, 0.097].
     - Positions made only of common entries: rarest (square, piece) count of 100k or more gives 0.065
       [0.051, 0.079].
   - Largest gaps:
     - Where the two models' most likely class differs (20% of positions): 0.198 [0.129, 0.268].
     - Long games: ply 251 and up gives 0.234 [0.118, 0.349].
     - Bare endgames: 2-6 pieces gives 0.147 [0.111, 0.184], and 0.227 for the league pair.
     - Drawn games: 0.143 [0.091, 0.194].
   - Rarity does not favour NB. Positions with an entry seen only 598-999 times in training (2,376) give 0.162
     [0.032, 0.293]. Training saw every possible (square, piece) entry at least 598 times; the rarest is a white
     king on a8. So nothing in the test set is truly rare.
   - Side to move makes no difference: 0.111 against 0.110.
   - Result: black win 0.100, white win 0.102, draw 0.143, all above zero. These bins use the label, so a gate
     cannot use them.

3. Gate (gate.out)
   Each part is scored by a gate fitted on the other fold. Both calibrations and the gate are fitted on that
   fold too. The mix is p = g LR + (1-g) NB, and the settings were fixed before any held-out loss was seen.

     method      held-out log-loss  minus LR alone [95% CI]   winner  minus LR [95% CI]
     LR alone    0.6234                                         0.3299
     NB alone    0.7338   0.1104 [ 0.0785,  0.1423]             0.4056   0.0757 [ 0.0429,  0.1084]
     constant    0.6237   0.0003 [-0.0009,  0.0014]             0.3301   0.0002 [-0.0012,  0.0015]
     outputs     0.6228  -0.0006 [-0.0036,  0.0024]             0.3302   0.0003 [-0.0024,  0.0030]
     full gate   0.6232  -0.0002 [-0.0046,  0.0041]             0.3307   0.0008 [-0.0030,  0.0045]
     oracle      0.5142  -0.1092 [-0.1240, -0.0944]             0.2606  -0.0692 [-0.0834, -0.0551]

   - constant: one weight for every position. The fitted weight on LR is 0.992 on fold A and 0.925 on fold B.
   - outputs: a sigmoid over both models' calibrated log-probabilities and confidences.
   - full gate: outputs plus the 70 features one-hot, 870 weights. It is an LR over the position and both
     outputs.
   - The full gate cut the training loss of its own fold by about 0.003, but none of that holds out. Where NB
     beats LR on a position is not predictable from the position or from either model's output.
   - The oracle is only a loose bound. It picks the better model after seeing the result. Wherever two models
     disagree, one of them always looks better on the outcome that happened, even when their expected losses are
     equal.
   - To check this, the same oracle over two LRs (lr-all and the league's F-L-c, 0.6234 and 0.6354) scores
     0.5528, a "gain" of 0.071 (oracle_check.out). The NB oracle's larger gain (0.109) mostly reflects that NB
     disagrees with LR more, not that it is right more often.

Caveats
- This measures prediction loss on league games (round 84), not playing strength.
- NB gets one global 4-parameter calibration. Its raw draw slope is about 0.1, so it is wildly overconfident.
  A calibration per group might narrow some gaps. That was not tried, but the gate could already lean on NB
  per group and found nothing.
- The league pair (F-N-c, F-L-c) was trained on other self-play data, which may include round-84-like
  positions. Both are low-capacity models and the picture matches the offline pair.
