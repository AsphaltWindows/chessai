Q9: where the league's CPU goes, how much of it is movegen, and whether a position/move cache pays at our depths
2026-11-09 01:30-03:00, models/from-scratch/movegen/. All runs nice 19, 1 thread; about 20 CPU min in all.

IN SHORT
- The tournament is 70% of the league's CPU (cycle 49: 11,566 of 16,552 CPU s), self-play 22% (a fixed budget),
  gates, own-cal and own steps 8%. About 17% of the league's CPU is movegen.
- Movegen is 19% of the live tournament's CPU, 33% of a d2 flat-LR game, 15% of a d2 tree/MoE game, 24% at d1 and 8% in
  d0 self-play. At d2, 96% of the movegen calls are at leaves, and they build the whole legal-move list only to see
  whether the side to move is mated or stalemated.
- A position or eval cache doesn't pay at depths 0-2. A 2-ply search hardly transposes (0.02% of evals repeat within
  a search; in 2 plies only a promotion captured at once, promoted to different pieces, can). Consecutive searches
  share 0-0.4% of evals, a game 0.6-2.4%, and a whole run 1.9-9% (openings). A probe costs 3-19 ns against evals
  of 0.2-1.2 us, so the net is between -1% and 2%. From d3 on it changes: 14% of evals repeat within a d3 search
  and 31% within a d4 one. Engines cache because they search deep.
- What pays, all exact (byte-identical games, checked): (1) a cheap "any legal move?" test at leaves instead of the
  full list, (2) not building every move's candidate encoding when the mover searches, (3) encoding from the piece
  bitboards. Together: 44% less CPU at d1, 46% at d2 with flat LR/probit, 21% at d2 with a tree and a MoE, and
  17% in d0 self-play (encode alone). Estimated for the tournament: about 25% (20-30%) less CPU, i.e. ~2,900 CPU s
  and ~9 minutes of wall time less per cycle.
- The next lever is NNUE-style incremental sums for the additive parts (LR, probit, NB, MoE experts and gates, and
  per-node caches for trees). It could take off roughly another third of the tournament, but it changes rounding, so
  games won't be byte-identical (unless the sums go fixed-point, which is a one-time arithmetic change).

   Where the movegen is:
   - d2: 96% of calls at leaves (7.38M of 7.69M in 120 games), the rest depth-1 nodes, roots and Game::result().
     A d2 search has ~25 root moves x ~26 replies = 662 leaves, all evaluated: root_values gives every root move a
     full window, so nothing is pruned.
   - d1: leaves 87% of calls; the rest candidates() (every ply, wasted when the mover searches) and result().
   - d0 self-play: 4.5% is movegen inside the SAN move text (plus 0.9% writing it), which --no-games throws away;
     and result() and candidates() generate the same position's list twice each ply (3.2% for both).
   - Per leaf (CPU): d2 LR-probit 0.66 us (eval part: predict + calibration + log-odds 0.27 us); d2 tree-MoE 1.67 us
     (1.22 us); d1 0.81 us (0.29 us). The full legal-move list at a leaf costs about 0.2 us.
   - Cross-check: the perf runs' sampled CPU matches the league binary's timed CPU (d2 LR-probit 40 vs 41 ms/game,
     d1 1.63 vs 1.69, d0 self-play 2.1 vs 2.0 s/round; tree-MoE 158 vs 167 ms/game on a busier machine). The
     instrumented build with every switch off is 2-11% slower than the league binary (frame pointers, flag checks),
     so section 5 compares each option with both.

   Probe cost (bench/probe.rs, thread CPU time): a direct-mapped table keyed by the Zobrist hash the search already
   carries costs 1-4 ns a probe at 128 KB-2 MB and 13-19 ns at 32-128 MB in a tight loop (a probe that can't overlap
   its DRAM miss costs more, up to ~80-100 ns). Hashing the 70-byte encoding instead: ~30 ns.
   Net per eval at d<=2, with a whole-run table: d2 LR saves 2.7% x 0.27 us = 7 ns against ~15 ns (loss); d2 tree-MoE
   saves 1.9% x 1.22 us = 23 ns (gain about 0.5% of a leaf); d1 saves 9% x 0.29 us = 26 ns (gain about 1.5%); d0
   self-play saves 5% x 0.2 us = 10 ns (loss). A legal-move-list cache has nothing left to save once leaves stop
   building lists (the remaining full lists are 4% of d2 calls).
   At d3 and d4 an eval cache would save 14-31% of evals, and a transposition table more (it stores search results,
   so it skips whole subtrees). Our only d4 users are F-L-d4mix-c and F-L-d4jit-c, about 50 CPU s each per cycle. A
   transposition table there wouldn't be exact either: draws by repetition and the 50-move rule depend on the path.

4. WHAT MATURE ENGINES CACHE (sources at the end)
   - Stockfish's transposition table stores search results: a 10-byte entry (16-bit key, depth, generation/bound,
     best move, search value, static eval), 3 to a 32-byte cluster, replaced by depth and age. It pays through
     transpositions and iterative-deepening re-searches at depth 10-40, and through move ordering (the stored best
     move is tried first, so alpha-beta cuts more). The static-eval field makes it an eval cache on the side.
   - Separate eval caches ("evaluation hash tables") were used for expensive hand-written evals. Forum reports put the
     gain at ~20% for a big eval and ~6% once lazy eval was in; the rule is that a probe must cost less than the eval.
     Pawn and material hash tables cached the parts of the classical eval that depend on a few pieces only (pawn
     structure, material balance), with very high hit rates. That eval was removed from Stockfish in July 2023 (SF 16).
   - NNUE: the first layer is linear in sparse piece-square features, so make/unmake adds or subtracts a few weight
     columns instead of recomputing it (the "efficiently updatable" part). A king move refreshes its half (features
     are king-relative); "Finny tables" (Luecx, Koivisto; now Stockfish's accumulator caches) keep a per-king-bucket
     copy of the accumulator and its board, so a refresh is a diff against the cached board.
   - Lc0 caches network outputs (NNCache, default 200,000 positions) separately from its search tree, which it reuses
     between moves. A GPU eval costs far more than a probe, and MCTS revisits positions.
   - Perft programs hash heavily (50-80% faster), because perft walks every transposition.
   - Nobody caches move lists. Engines make moves pseudo-legally and lazily (staged generation: hash move, captures,
     quiets; legality checked when a move is tried), and they don't build a leaf's move list to test for mate.
   What applies here: no transposition table at d<=2 (nothing transposes, nothing is re-searched, and full windows
   give alpha-beta nothing to cut). The engines' lesson that does apply is laziness: don't build lists or encodings
   nobody uses, and update the evaluation incrementally.

   Incremental evaluation for our models. All of the following are sums over the 70 categorical features:
   - LR and probit (linear predictor), NB/CNBP (log-likelihood sums): one accumulator per output. A move changes 3-4
     bytes (from, to, side to move, en passant; castling and en passant captures occasionally), so a child costs ~4
     lookups per output instead of 70.
   - MoE (e.g. M-L4-N: 4 LR experts and an NB gate): one accumulator set per expert and gate, each updated the same
     way.
   - Trees and DAGs: the route can change between parent and child, so keep a Finny-style cache per node (the encoding
     it last saw and its sums). A node's eval is then a 70-byte diff against that, plus a few deltas. Siblings in a
     search differ by one move, so the diffs are small. TAN routers also need the changed squares' children terms. FM
     needs its per-factor sums.
   - The nonlinear tail stays per position: softmax/link, own calibration, log-odds (11% of the tournament).
   - Exactness: float sums in a different order round differently, so games are not byte-identical to today's. Sums
     in fixed point (as NNUE's int16/int32) are order-independent and exact, but that's a one-time arithmetic change
     for every player.

RECOMMENDATION
- Build 1-3, always on, into a copy of chessai-hybrid3's source as the tournament binary. Check byte-identical games
  against chessai-hybrid3 on a sample of the real field (one round-robin of ~20 entries of every kind), then switch at
  a cycle boundary. That's about a quarter off the tournament, the biggest consumer, with identical results.
- Gates, own-cal and self-play run on each lineage's own binary (14 binaries from different source trees). They gain
  when those are rebuilt with the same patch (search.rs, selfplay.rs, encoding.rs, player.rs), and rs/ should get it
  too. Caveat for self-play: its budget is fixed, so faster self-play means more games per cycle, and unevenly (d2
  lineages gain 20-45%, d0 trees less). That's a one-time change in the experiment's conditions, unlike the
  tournament speedup, which only saves time.
- Don't build an eval, position or move-list cache for depths 0-2. Revisit only if d3+ play becomes common.
- Incremental sums are the largest remaining lever, but they change rounding. That's the user's call; fixed-point
  sums would make them exact from then on.

METHOD AND FILES
- src-perf/: copy of chessai-hybrid3's source (hybrid2-src) with frame pointers and line tables; plays identical games.
  perf/: profiles (w1 d0 self-play F-L-c 6 rounds; w2 d1 LR v probit 6000 games; w3 d2 LR v probit 500; w4 d2 tree v MoE
  100; tournament-flat, 30 s of cycle 49's tournament), tools/attribute.py and tools/leafsyms.py.
- src-instr/: src-perf plus src/instr.rs, every switch off by default (plays as chessai-hybrid3): Q9_COUNT (counters
  and cache bookkeeping), Q9_FASTLEAF, Q9_LAZYCAND, Q9_ENCODE2, Q9_CHECK (asserts each fast path against the original
  on every call). The fast leaf test: if not in check, any unpinned knight, pawn (push or capture) or slider move, or
  a king step to a square no enemy piece attacks, proves a legal move; in check, a safe king step does. Otherwise it
  falls back to the full list (0.17-0.34% of leaves).
- Validation (tools/validate.sh): with all fast paths and asserts on, d1 (1500 games), d2 LR-probit (120) and d2
  tree-MoE (24) arenas wrote byte-identical games to chessai-hybrid3's (12.7M leaves asserted), and a d0 self-play
  round trained a byte-identical model.
- The league entries used: F-L-c v4753/v4778, F-P-tau0-c v4134, T-L-Ne-c v1878, M-L4-N-c v338, with their own-cal
  games (snapshots in snap/: the league pruned v4753's own-cal games mid-run).
- Nothing in rs/, the league, the staged dirs, or models/from-scratch/bin was touched.
