- The flags apply to bdt and bdd training (train and self-play).

1. --error-target gap (a new ErrorTarget), with --target-gap-cap C (default 4)
   - Per example, with the children's errors l_k: w_k = min(l_max - l_k, C).
   - If sum(w) = 0 (all the errors tie, as for the identical copies right after a split), the router does not train.
     The example is still routed and trains a child.
   - Otherwise the router takes ONE weighted step on the label w/sum(w), with weight sum(w):
     - CNBP: adds w_k to class k's counts (fractional counts).
     - LR: one AdaGrad step with gradient sum(w) p - w. Never B separate steps.
   - TAN routers are refused: "--error-target gap needs LR or CNBP error routers (a TAN router trains on unit counts
     only)". A TAN's NB part counts every example as 1. Probit routers are refused by the same check.
   - For B = 2 it is the pairwise (TAO) gap: the better child, weighted by |l_1 - l_2|, and nothing on a tie.
   - It applies wherever error routers train:
     - --route error;
     - the error router of --route cluster --route-switch, both while it is compared and after the switch;
     - BDD routers and their router-split sub-routers;
     - BDD delegator trials.
   - router_error under gap (the window statistic that drives the split triggers): the weighted error of the step.
     - That is sum(w) times the router's error against w/sum(w). With --error log, it is sum_k w_k (-ln p_k).
     - It is 0 for a silent step.
     - It is the loss the weighted step descends.
     - It is recorded for every scored example, as before, so windows close at the same pace and the counters keep
       their meaning.
     - Its scale is not Best's: it is in nats of gap, and near 0 while the children are alike. So router-window settings
       tuned under Best do not carry over.
   - Refused: --target-gap-cap without gap, or a cap of 0 or less.

2. --route-before-train
   - The example trains the child the error router picked before its update (theory 4.1). Prediction is unchanged.
   - Only for error routers: --route error, or --route cluster with --route-switch (after the switch). Refused otherwise.

3. --split-bootstrap N (default 0)
   - A router made by a leaf split under --route error sends its first N scored examples (and any unscored ones meanwhile)
     to the child a hash picks.
     - The hash is position_hash (the one cluster_pick uses), mixed with the node's index by a splitmix64 finaliser. So
       different delegators split the same positions differently.
     - It is deterministic.
   - The router still trains on its targets as usual.
   - The count is the node's num_trained_total. That counter is already saved and restarts at 0 at the split, so nothing
     new is saved and a resume continues the count.
   - Refused for BDDs: router splits make copies whose counters restart.
   - Refused for cluster routing: cluster routers have their own hash start (threshold/5).
   - In the mechanism runs, N = 100,000, one split window (theory 9.2: "route the first window of a new router by a
     hash"). The presplit precedent used threshold/5.

- Not exercised by real runs: the gap path of BDD delegator trials. Unit tests cover it, but the identity run's only two
  trials were undone.

Against the predictions:
1. "Gap lowers U for LR routers": seen.
   - Under Best, U is 0.14-0.16. That is the bottom of tonight's league range for LR error routers (0.14-0.53).
   - With gap, U is 0.07-0.11 in all three gap arms at every checkpoint. That is inside the range of NB routers
     (0.02-0.12) and cluster routers (0.03-0.11).
   - The leaves carry less information about who is winning: I(leaf; Ebin) falls from 0.28-0.31 to 0.16-0.26, while
     H(leaf) rises.
   - CNBP routers are already low under Best (0.08-0.10). With gap and the bootstrap, U is 0.03 and 0.07 at rounds 20
     and 40, and about the same at 60 (0.09 against 0.08).
2. "Gap avoids lock-in to one leaf": mostly seen, for LR routers.
   - Best's LR routers send 93-98% of the test positions to one child at three delegators by round 60.
   - Gap alone lowers the mean largest share from 74-75% to 53-58%, but 2-3 delegators still starve a child.
   - With the bootstrap, no delegator starves a child (largest share 44-49%). Effective leaves on the test traffic rise
     from 8.5-9.9 to 12.4-17.5 of 21.
   - CNBP under Best was already fairly even (47-51%). One delegator has kept its middle child at 1% since its split at
     round 7.
   - CNBP with gap and the bootstrap: 44-47%, with the root's third child at 2%. Theory 3.1 allows a starved leaf to be
     the correct attractor.
3. "Without the bootstrap, splits use their children one at a time": not seen under gap; seen under Best. Source:
   analysis.txt sections 2b and 2c, the children's training counts after each split.
   - lr-gap (no bootstrap): every one of its 10 splits had all three children training at the first checkpoint after the
     split. The least-used child had 14-33% of the first 3-42k examples.
   - Best: 7 of 10 LR-router splits and 8 of 10 CNBP-router splits started with a child at 0-1% of the examples.
     - That child took 1-8 rounds to reach 10%.
     - Two CNBP delegators were still under 10% after 8 rounds.
     - nb-best's delegator 1 kept its middle child at 1% for 53 rounds.
   - A likely reason (not measured per example):
     - Gap is silent only until the first child trains. After that, the router routes by its updated pick, and a fresh
       LR router's pick follows each step (theory 4.2: the leak is total for a fresh router). So the outcome noise in
       the realised gaps breaks the remaining tie within a few examples.
     - Under Best, ties go to the first child, so the untrained copy's logit falls on every step until that copy is
       strictly best.
     - Gap with route-before-train but without the bootstrap was not an arm. Without the leak, the one-at-a-time
       pattern may appear there.
4. Offline loss (a sanity check only: offline loss does not predict play): no new option beats Best.
   - Gap costs 0.002-0.015 nats with LR routers.
   - The bootstrap on top of gap costs more at rounds 40 and 60.
   - Route-before-train wins back 0.005-0.015 nats at all three checkpoints, which fits the leak of 4.1 costing loss.
     The full combination is level with Best at round 40 and 0.005 behind at 60 (an interval that touches 0).
   - CNBP with gap and the bootstrap is worse at rounds 20 and 40, and level at 60.
5. Also seen:
   - Under gap, the LR trees' leaves disagree more: the mean |log-odds difference| of a pair of leaves is 1.3-2.0, against
     0.7 under Best. CNBP trees: 0.74-0.97 against 0.78-0.82.
   - The bootstrap's 100k examples split each new router's data into thirds by hash. Deeper routers took up to 8 rounds
     to finish it: 3 of lr-gap-boot-rbt's 10 delegators were still inside it at round 40, and none at 60.
