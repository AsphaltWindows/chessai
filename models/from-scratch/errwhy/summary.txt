errwhy (2026-11-07): why error routers predict better but play worse, and how to turn the prediction into play.
Diagnostics only: the judges and the outside reference never label anything a lineage trains on. No new chessai
binary (tool/ is a diagnostic crate on lrshare-src, md5 of its build below). League models copied from cycles/runs
(read only) into models/. Everything ran at nice 19 with at most 2 threads.
CPU: A 0.5 min, C 1.5, B 11.1 (with two trials), C2 1.6, D 7.0: about 21.7 CPU-min in all.

SHORT ANSWERS
1. Too weak, or the target? Both, in that order, but routers are not where the trees lose.
   - The best-child target is biased towards the result. Routers retrained on held-out league positions with it never
     beat the trees' own routers (any kind: CNBP, LR, TAN, FM). Their picks share more information with the game's
     result than an expected-loss rule's (mutual information at the root up to 0.10 nats, against 0.05).
   - The same LR router regressing each child's expected loss recovers 51% of the reachable gain on T-L-Ne-c and 20%
     on T-L-Le-c.
   - CNBP and TAN routers can't use even a clean target (21% at most on LR-leaf trees). FM with a clean target gets
     50-74%. So capacity binds for naive-Bayes-type routers.
   - Even reference-quality routing at every router would only bring T-L-Ne-c up to flat LR on league positions. The
     leaves bind.
2. Where does the prediction gain go?
   - CNBP error routers' gains are measured on their own training stream, and don't reach league positions. There
     T-L-Ne-c and T-N-Ne-c predict worse than their cluster twins (0.014 and 0.039 nats).
   - LR error routers do predict better than their cluster twins. That shows at ply 2: both judges rate their moves
     better, and the league agrees.
   - At ply 1 the gain is lost, mostly at seams. The 1-ply argmax over hard-routed siblings takes moves whose
     position falls in another leaf that rates them 0.5-0.7 nats above the parent's leaf. In the 20% of positions
     where T-L-Le-c's top move crosses, it gives up 0.011-0.013 of decisive win probability per move against
     T-L-Nc-c.
3. What should go in the league?
   - Soft routing at play time (blend), for LR-router error trees, at ply 1. Blending a tree's leaves by its routers'
     probabilities, against the same tree routed hard, gains 95 Elo (85 to 106) for T-L-Le-c and 75 (64 to 85) for
     T-L-Le-fresh-c at ply 1 (2000 games each).
     Re-judged in E against the right comparator, the same tree calibrated on its own games (cal-own): 58 and 45.
   - Blending carries that, not the leaves' recalibration: recalibration alone against hard is 1, -8, 12 and 1 Elo.
   - The ply-2 gains (55 and 35 Elo) are a separate effect: recalibrating the whole tree alone gives the same 56.
     That is the league's fixed 0.1-nat softmax acting on underconfident log-odds, not routing.
   - Cost: about 4-5x CPU per game.

B. TOO WEAK, OR THE TARGET? (errwhy routers -> b_TREE/, b_TREE/summary.txt, b_overview.txt)
Setup. Each delegator's children are frozen. Its router is retrained on the positions of league round-robins 80-83
that reach it (every 4th position, 577k at the root; 2 passes for LR and FM). Every rule is scored on round-robin
84's parts with a calibration fitted per rule and part.
Targets:
- best: the child of least 3-class log-loss on the result, as --route error --error log.
- soft: responsibilities.
- clean: the child closest to an outside LR (offline-c15's LR trained on rounds 80-83). Diagnostic only.
- costsens and regress (LR form, expected loss): a softmax gate minimising the expected loss, and a regression of
  each child's loss minus the children's mean.
- ref plug-in (no training): picks the child closest to the outside LR's prediction at test time. It stands for the
  reachable gain.
Gain over the tree's own routers, summed over delegators weighted by their share of positions, calibrated, x1000
(share of the ref plug-in's gain):

- The trees' own routers (trained in self-play on far more positions) already match anything trained on the best

- For T-L-Ne-c the reachable gain (26) would take it from 0.643 to about 0.617: flat LR's level (0.619), not past it.

D. SMOKE: blend against hard, same tree (d_run.sh, d2_run.sh, d3_run.sh -> d_run.out, d2_run.out, d3_run.out, d_pool.py)
Setup. blend:1:GAMES:bdt:PATH, the main binary's kind. GAMES is the tree's own games: 600 games of itself at ply 1
with the league policy (d_games/, about 1 s CPU). Arenas use the league policy (log-odds softmax, T 0.1) on
chessai-lrshare.
Blend minus hard:
  T-L-Le-c        ply 1: 95 Elo (85 to 106), 2000 games; ply 2: 55 (29 to 82), 600 games
  T-L-Le-fresh-c  ply 1: 75 (64 to 85), 2000 games;     ply 2: 35 (9 to 62), 600 games
Separating the two parts of blend (500 games a run at ply 1, 200 at ply 2):
- ply 1, leafcal minus hard (recalibration only): 1, -8 (T-L-Le-c); 12, 1 (T-L-Le-fresh-c).
- ply 1, blend minus leafcal (blending only): 78, 70; 64, 54.
- ply 2, T-L-Le-c: leafcal minus hard 58 (13 to 105); blend minus leafcal -23 (-68 to 22).
- ply 2, T-L-Le-c, whole-tree recalibration (cal:GAMES): cal minus hard 56 (11 to 103); blend minus cal 30 (-16 to
  76). At ply 1, cal minus hard 26 (6 to 46).
So at ply 1 blending carries the gain. At ply 2 the gain is recalibration sharpening the league's fixed 0.1-nat
softmax for a tree whose own-game calibration slope is about 1.35. That would help any underconfident entry; it is
a league-policy effect, not routing.
Cost: blend plays a game in about 4-5x the CPU of hard routing at ply 1 and ply 2 (T-L-Le-c, one thread). It
evaluates every leaf whose blend weight is over 0.001. A larger cut-off would be cheaper (untested).
The league's tournament binary chessai-tanroute2 loads blend specs (checked).

E. BLEND RE-JUDGED AGAINST CAL-OWN (added after leaguecal; e_run.sh -> e_run.out, e_pool.py -> e_pool.txt)
Why. D compared blend with the same tree routed hard and uncalibrated. The league plays each tree's league spec
(cal:heldout-lr-d2-s555, calibrated on held-out d2 games). leaguecal showed that calibrating a tree on its own games
(cal-own) sharpens it under the league's 0.1-nat softmax, routing unchanged. So D's gains mix routing and sharpening.
Setup. As D: the same trees (T-L-Le-c-bdt1081, T-L-Le-fresh-c-bdt682), the same own games (d_games/),
chessai-lrshare (md5 e9ae9c6528edb207399a6e1b293278d5), league policy, alternating colours, two threads, nice 19.
Three specs per tree:
  league spec  cal:heldout-lr-d2-s555/round0:bdt:PATH
  cal-own      cal:d_games/TREE:bdt:PATH (the whole tree calibrated on its own games, routing hard)
  blend        blend:1:d_games/TREE:bdt:PATH (as in D)
Ply 1: 2000 games per pairing (direct blend - league spec: 1000). Ply 2: 600 games per pairing.

On one scale, league spec 0 (blend chained through cal-own):

                         cal-own       blend          cal-own       blend
  T-L-Le-c-bdt1081       23 (13-33)    81 (67-96)     45 (19-72)    87 (49-124)
  T-L-Le-fresh-c-bdt682  30 (21-40)    75 (61-89)     55 (28-81)    56 (19-94)
Blend minus cal-own, the right comparator:
  T-L-Le-c        ply 1: 58 (48 to 68); ply 2: 41 (15 to 68)
  T-L-Le-fresh-c  ply 1: 45 (35 to 55); ply 2: 1 (-25 to 28)
Direct check at ply 1, blend minus league spec: 73 (58 to 87) and 74 (59 to 88), against 81 and 75 chained.

Reading.
- At ply 1, blending is worth 45-58 Elo over the same tree on cal-own: about 60% of the 95 and 75 it gained over
  hard routing in D. The other 30-37 of D's gain was calibration, which cal-own gets without blending.
- At ply 2, the gain over the league spec is mostly cal-own (45-55). Blend adds 41 for T-L-Le-c and nothing for
  T-L-Le-fresh-c, so blend's ply-2 routing gain is uncertain.
- Against what the league plays now (the league spec), blend is 73-81 at ply 1 and 56-87 at ply 2.
- Cost: blend still takes 4-5x the CPU per game. cal-own costs nothing at play time; making its own games costs
  1-3 CPU-s per version (leaguecal).
- For a blend entry, the comparator should be the same version on cal-own. If the league adopts cal-own, blend's own
  contribution is 45-58 at ply 1 and 0-41 at ply 2.
- Note: T-L-Le-fresh-c here is v682, the version behind D's 75. leaguecal measured the later v742 on
  chessai-tanroute2: cal-own minus league spec 29 at ply 1 and 69 at ply 2, in line with v682's 30 and 55.
