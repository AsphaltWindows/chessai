Topic "assign" (2026-11-08): epsilon-random training assignment for error routers (--assign-epsilon E).

Question (from eloss and leshare): error trees lose when the training router's pick narrows each leaf's data. The
best-child target's noise and leaf sharing both keep the leaves broad. Does sending a random share of the examples
to a random child do the same, and does it add anything to sharing?

Verdict: negative as a league line. No lineage.
- Offline it helps the unshared tree a little, about 40% of what sharing 0.3 does (E 0.2: -0.0027 [-0.0043,
  -0.0011]). On top of sharing 0.3 it adds nothing at any E.
- In play, the setting that passed offline (no sharing, E 0.2) is level with its comparator at equal data, pooled
  over five version pairs: 9 (-2 to 20) at ply 1 and -17 (-41 to 7) at ply 2.
- So it doesn't substitute for sharing, and there is nothing to add to T-L-Le-share3-c.

Code: ../assign-src = ../sparsefit-src + assign.patch (assign-src/assign.patch).
- DelegationParams::assign_epsilon. In Bdt::train_node, each delegator's router trains on the example as before;
  then Graph::assigned_child sends the example, with probability E, to a child drawn uniformly (possibly the
  router's own pick) instead of the router's pick. The error passed down is the error of the child that trains.
  Both the scored path (error and cluster routing) and the unscored path draw.
- The draw hashes the step count and the node index (splitmix64), so it uses no random state and is the same on
  every run. The step count is in the state files, so resumed runs don't repeat draws. At E = 0 nothing is drawn.
- Prediction, play and the model files are unchanged. State files record the option only when it is nonzero (the
  params' Debug), so states without it are what they were.
- CLI: --assign-epsilon E (0 to 1), --model bdt only (checked for train and self-play).
- Four new unit tests: the examples spread evenly at E = 1 where the router sends more than 90% to one child at 0;
  the draw moves an example with probability E (k - 1) / k and never at 0; the option is recorded only when used;
  E must be a probability. 139 tests pass.

- Does epsilon substitute for sharing? No. Against the sharing trees:
  - le-e20 - le-s03: 0.0036 [0.0014, 0.0058], winner 0.0023 [0.0005, 0.0041];
  - le-e05 / le-e10 / le-e20 - le-s01: 0.0030 [0.0004, 0.0057] / 0.0035 [0.0013, 0.0057] / 0.0020 [-0.0002, 0.0043].
  - Against flat LR (0.6116), le-e20 is 0.0015 [-0.0004, 0.0034] worse; le-s03 is 0.0021 [0.0010, 0.0031] better.
- Leaves: 9 in every unshared tree; 11, 11 and 9 in the shared ones (le-s03: 11).
- Reading:
  - The direction supports the hypothesis: broader leaf data helps error trees. Leaf disagreement falls (0.50 to
    0.42) and the root's routing regret against each group's best child falls from 0.0028 to 0.0000-0.0007, because
    the children are more alike.
  - But a random tenth or fifth of the examples is a weak way to broaden the leaves. Sharing gives every leaf a
    weighted step on every example, and it cuts the disagreement to 0.15. Once the leaves share, the random
    assignment's extra breadth is redundant (all level, intervals about 0.001 wide).
  - The dose response is flat within the noise (0.05: 0.0017, 0.1: 0.0012, 0.2: 0.0027); about 0.001 per part is
    the noise here.
  - It doesn't touch what eloss found: that the router's pick also assigns the training data. Epsilon only dilutes
    the assignment; the play router is the same router.
