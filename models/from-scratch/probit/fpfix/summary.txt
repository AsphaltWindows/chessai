F-P self-play randomness: diagnosis, fix and check (2026-11-07). Verdict: the fix fails (about -350 to -430 Elo); do not deploy.

Diagnosis (chessai-probit4 probit-diag: 100 games of the model's own jitter10 self-play, seed 3; diag-1646.txt,
diag-others.txt). F-P-c probit1646: learned draw margin eps 1.988, beta 1; the weights' variance at a candidate is
1.30e-3 at the median and 1.33e-3 at most, so S/beta = 1.0006 at every candidate.
  variant                                    top-two E gap median (90%)   jitter10 non-top
  as is                                      1.4e-3 (6.9e-3)              83.3%
  mean weights (no variance inflation)       1.4e-3 (6.9e-3)              83.4%
  eps fitted to the games' draw share 1.980  1.4e-3 (7.0e-3)              83.2%
  eps 0 / 0.5 / 1                            7.2e-3 / 6.5e-3 / 4.8e-3     62.3% / 64.5% / 70.3%
  (LR, F-L-c lr3143: 7.1e-3, 60.7%)
Q1: the draw band compresses the gaps, and the inflation contributes nothing measurable. eps is not mis-estimated: it
    matches the self-play draw share. E's slope in m is phi(eps/S)/S = 0.055 at eps 2, against 0.40 at eps 0.
    eps now: about 2.0 in self-play (1.960 at v1808; 1.93-2.13 over v1770-v1808). The own-step versions, trained on d2
    league games, sit at 0.33-0.40 (v166, v909, v1262, v1459, v1809), and one self-play round brings eps back to ~2.
Q2: no. The inflation changes the top log-odds move in 0.00% of positions (S is the same at every candidate), and so do
    all the eps variants. eps scales the log-odds, which the tournament's calibration slope refits anyway.
Q3: the proposed fix, chessai-probit4 --probit-play-eps 0. Self-play chooses moves with copies of the models at draw
    margin 0. Training, the learned eps, the model files and the tournament are unchanged.

Check (chessai-probit4; from scratch, seed 21, 1000 games/round, --vs-previous, 100 rounds, nice 19, 2 threads; run.sh):
  The baseline reproduces chessai-probit's F-P-s21 v1-v30 byte for byte.
  CPU: FP-base 163.3 s (25.3M plies), FP-fix 113.6 s (19.8M plies): 30% cheaper, because its games are shorter
  (191 vs 241 plies a game in rounds 90-99). Self-play draws in rounds 60-99: base 88.3%, fix 95.9%.
  Learned eps at v100: base 1.955, fix 2.252.
  Equal CPU (cumulative plies x CPU per ply): fix v100 (113.6 s) ~ base v68 (112.9 s).

Why (play-diag, chessai-diag2 = chessai-probit4 + play-diag.patch, analysis only; 1000 games, same model both sides,
seed 3; play-diag.txt):
                                  jitter10 non-top   decisive   plies/game   draws by repetition
  F-L-c lr3143                    60.6%              4.9%       152          11.8%
  F-P-c probit1646, as is         83.9%              12.1%      234          2.4%
  probit1646, play eps 0          66.7%              4.1%       178          10.2%
  FP-base v100                    83.4%              9.9%       247          2.9%
  FP-fix v100, play eps 0         61.5%              3.4%       207          36.4%
  eps 0 is greedier in every material bucket, including when the mover is 3+ points ahead (76.6% vs 88.6% non-top for
  probit1646). So this is plain greediness, not random play when winning. LR-like move choice gives the probit a third of
  the decisive games and many more repetitions, and its models end up far weaker. F-P's random move choice is not what
  keeps it below LR; the tested direction (less random) costs about 400 Elo.
