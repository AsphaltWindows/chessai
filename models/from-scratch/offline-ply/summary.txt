Offline discriminator of theory-2 6.35 (11-11 about 17:10-20:50; fork of the main session). Question: do ply-2 games hold the
structure the probit error tree exploits at ply 1 (H1: no, the field's play removes it; H2: yes, the league loop is
slow)? Everything is in models/from-scratch/offline-ply/.

METHOD
- Players: G2's finals (error-routing-p1 v314: F-P#1, F-P#2, T-P-Ne#1, T-P-Ne#2) and probit-late-p2's finals (v399,
  the same four kinds). Each plays with its own calibration as in the leagues: the 8 own-calibration game sets were
  recreated with the leagues' exact arena (600 games, depth 1, log-odds softmax T 0.1, seed 5), and all 8 results
  are identical to the leagues' .arena records (owncal.py).
- Pools: round robins (chessai-hybrid15 tournament --save-games, log-odds softmax T 0.1, --use-probs) of the four.
  - P1 = G2 at ply 1, seed 61001.
  - P2 = late-p2 at ply 2, seed 61002.
  - P2x = G2 at ply 2, seed 61003.
  - P1x = late-p2 at ply 1, seed 61004.
  - Generated until at least 24M positions (games.csv plies). Sizes: P1 24.7M and P1x 27.6M (213,600 games
    each), P2 27.7M and P2x 38.0M (262,800 games each). players-*.txt, gen.py.
- Split (split.py): a seeded shuffle of each pool's games (seed 7001). The last 10% is held out (10 rounds, by game);
  the rest forms training rounds of about 200k positions (about one league cycle).
- Training (train.py, chessai-hybrid6, from scratch, one pass): the first 108 rounds, i.e. EQUAL positions, 21.61M in
  every pool. F-P: --model probit --use-probs. T-P-Ne: the fields' exact train_args (--leaf probit --branch-factor 3
  --split-threshold 1000000 --split-limit 4 --forget-factor 1 --router cnbp --route error --error log --reset-leaves
  inf --use-probs --split-noise 0.03). --seed 7101 for both models: the same order.
- Scoring (score.py):
  - chessai-treeview2 node-dump of both models streamed on the same held-out positions. Cross-check: eval-loss on the
    same positions gives the same g within 0.0003 on all 8 pairs (calib.json g_raw).
  - Full log-loss per position, g = flat minus tree (nats). SE clustered by game. Winner log-loss on decisive positions
    is secondary.
  - Per leaf: g within the tree's leaf; leaves labelled by mean non-pawn material (npm, 62 at the start) and men.
- Calibrated scorings (calib.py): the raw TRANSFERS are dominated by base-rate shift between pools; a flat model's
  log-loss on the other pool is 1.12-1.33, above ln 3 = 1.10. So each model also gets a 4-parameter calibration
  fitted on each scored held-out part (eval-loss --fit-calibration on the 10 merged parts; in-sample on 250-380k
  positions a part, negligible bias), and g is recomputed. SE over the 10 parts.
- The replicate demanded by 6.35 (g2/g1 0.654 ± 0.026 was within 2 SE of 2/3): second training seed 7102 on P1 and
  P2. train --seed redraws the within-round position order (rs/src/main.rs train_rounds); the across-round game order
  (7001) was not redrawn.

VERDICT AGAINST 6.35
- Not H1. H1 needs g2/g1 <= 1/3 and the P1 tree keeping <= 1/3 of g1 on P2. We have g2/g1 0.52-0.65 (0.58 over two
  seeds) and a calibrated transfer of 0.53 g1.
- Not cleanly H2. H2 needs g2/g1 >= 2/3 and a transfer >= 2/3. Seed 7101's 0.654 is within SE of 2/3, but seed 7102's
  0.516 is not, and the calibrated transfer is 0.53.
- So: BETWEEN, toward the H2 side. Ply-2 data keeps about 0.5-0.65 of the ply-1 headroom a one-pass tree can find on
  equal positions, and a ply-1-trained tree's correction is half a correction for ply-2 outcomes.
- 6.35's rule: expect a ply-2 edge of at most about 118 x g2/g1, i.e. 61-77 Elo (68 at the mean ratio 0.58), and
  "arm 1 is still worth one field".
- Depth and players (calibrated g / g1): changing either the depth (P2x 0.56) or the players (P1x 0.63) costs about
  40%; changing both (P2 0.65) costs no more. P1 (ply-1-trained players at ply 1) is the outlier with extra headroom.
  6.35's "depth sets the headroom" (P2x and P1x within 0.002 of P2 and P1) fails on its own numbers: P2x is 0.007-0.008
  below P2 (8% of g1, near only if 0.002 is scaled by the 8-15 times larger g1), and P1x is 0.032 below P1 (36% of g1).
  The players matter about as much as the depth.
- Side readouts: the P2 tree's leaf count is 9 and 7 (H1 said 5-7, H2 said 9), with the 4th split late or absent
  against v70-80 at ply 1. The per-leaf readout does not separate: P2's lowest-material leaves hold 57% (one) or 68%
  (three, npm <= 19) of g2, about H1's 60% line, but P1 itself holds 68% in its lowest leaf.
- Prediction misses:
  - g1 came in at 0.088-0.095, against 0.006-0.012 predicted (8-15 times larger).
  - g2 came in at 0.049-0.058, against 0.001-0.004 (ratio 0.15-0.4 predicted).
  - The P1 tree on P2 kept 0.53 (calibrated), against under 0.3 predicted.
  - The P2 tree on P1 kept 0.84, against 0.3-0.6.
  - The winner-loss gains are not "about equal" to the full-loss gains on P2.
  - Partly right: P2's splits come later (one P2 tree stopped at 7 leaves), and the gain is endgame-concentrated.
- Caveat: these are log-loss gains of one-pass offline training on fixed players' games, not Elo. The leagues' trees
  also train on their own changing play.
