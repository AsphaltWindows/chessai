bddstudy: what router splits do in the BDD (2026-11-08)

VERDICT
Router splits, as built, do nothing useful, and nothing harmful.
- A split lowers the root's measured regret for a round or two, but the tree's loss does not change.
- The sub-routers start as copies and diverge slowly. Each keeps the counts it was copied with, and afterwards trains
  only on what R' routes to it.
- R' does not learn to use them. Its routing beats always using its most-used sub-router in one seed and loses to it
  in the other.
- The BDD equals a BDT with the same 9 leaves on held-out RR 84: log-loss +0.0010 [-0.0002, +0.0021] at round 60, mean
  of two seeds. Its tie with the 21-leaf BDT (+0.0000 [-0.0023, +0.0024] here, as in bddcheck) comes from having fewer
  leaves. Router splits used 6 of the 10 splits, and 21 leaves are no better than 9 on this data:
  - BDT-4 minus BDT -0.0010 [-0.0032, +0.0012] at round 60;
  - -0.0097 [-0.0124, -0.0070] at round 40, while the BDT's late leaves were still fresh.
League: keep D-L-Ne-c as it is. At v61 it has 3 leaves and no router split. Its root's last closed router-error window
is 3.37 nats, and the current window is at 2.13 after 1.71M of its 5M positions, so the earliest a router split can
happen is when that window closes. Until then it is T-L-Ne-c's twin, and changing one of its factors would test
nothing about router splits.
Next one-factor test (offline; needs a code change in a source copy): train R' on its sub-routers' prediction errors
instead of their routing errors.
- Prediction errors: the loss of the leaf each sub-router would route to, as a BDT router trains on its children's.
- Routing errors: each sub-router's log-loss at predicting the best child.
- Why: CNBP routing errors ran 1.29-2.07 nats before the bdd splits, above a uniform guess (ln 3 = 1.10). R''s
  current target is mostly the sub-routers' overconfidence, not which leaf they pick.

SETUP
offline-c15:
- Training: RRs 80-83 (2,308,895 positions) in 60 rounds: 3 passes x 4 RRs x 5 chunks, with a fresh partition each
  pass (make_train.py; train/ is symlinks). A model was saved after every round.
- Test: RR 84 (575,099 positions in 10 parts).
- T-L-Ne-c's recipe: LR leaves, CNBP error routers, --error log, branch 3, forget 1, split threshold 100k, split
  limit 10, nice 19, single-threaded.
Arms (run.sh, run2.sh), seeds 1 and 2:
- bdt-s1/s2: chessai-cyc5 --model bdt.
- bdd-s1/s2: chessai-cyc5 --model bdd.
- bddbal-s1: chessai-bal --model bdd --error-target balanced. This is a second factor and exploratory.
- bdt4-s1/s2: the control, --split-limit 4, which gives a BDT the BDD's 9 leaves. It is identical to bdt-sN until
  that run's 5th split.
Tool: chessai-bddstudy, built from bddstudy-src = routercmp-src plus the bdd-stats command (bddstudy.patch).
- bdd-stats walks each test position down the graph by each router's own pick (pick_child).
- For every delegator it sums: positions; routed and best-child counts; routing regret against its children and
  against its base children (the shared children under a stack of sub-routers); the regret if one child took
  everything (single); and how often each pair of children reaches the same leaf (agree).
- Regrets are in raw 3-class log-loss per position.
- Identity (identity/): the binary retrains bddcheck's de-100k and ne-100k byte for byte, and its eval-loss and
  route-stats match chessai-routercmp's. On a BDT, bdd-stats reproduces route-stats.
Held-out losses: eval-loss with the calibration fitted on the other fold (lrnb's even/odd parts), with paired 95% t
intervals over the 10 parts (9 df).
Files:
- analysis.txt, from analyze.py. analysis-verbose.txt (--verbose) lists every R' node with each sub-router's regret
  alone.
- m/ARM/structure.json (structure.py) holds the split timeline and the per-node error windows for every round.
- stats/ARM-ROUND.out holds the bdd-stats output; cal.csv holds the held-out losses per part.

Q1. DOES SPLITTING A ROUTER REDUCE ROUTING REGRET?
The root's regret drops for a round or two, and the tree's loss does not drop with it.
- The first split was the root's, in round 4, in both seeds. Through round 3 the BDT is the same model as the BDD:
  only the root's window bookkeeping differs, and the classifiers are identical. So bdt-sN is the exact no-split
  counterfactual.
- Root regret is the routed child's loss minus the best of the root's three children's. BDD minus BDT, per test
  position (seed 1 / seed 2):
    round 4 (the split's): regret -0.0007 [-0.0010, -0.0003] / +0.0006 [-0.0002, +0.0015]
                           tree loss -0.0007 [-0.0011, -0.0003] / -0.0004 [-0.0012, +0.0003]
    round 5:               regret -0.0084 [-0.0093, -0.0075] / -0.0068 [-0.0087, -0.0049]
                           tree loss -0.0006 [-0.0015, +0.0004] / +0.0003 [-0.0008, +0.0015]
    round 6:               regret -0.0068 [-0.0097, -0.0038] / -0.0025 [-0.0061, +0.0012]
                           tree loss +0.0010 [-0.0010, +0.0030] / -0.0017 [-0.0032, -0.0001]
    round 8:               regret +0.0170 [+0.0117, +0.0224] / +0.0037 [-0.0011, +0.0084]
                           tree loss +0.0004 [-0.0038, +0.0046] / +0.0000 [-0.0028, +0.0029]
  - Root regret is 0.15-0.19 per position, so round 5's drop is about 5% of it. The best-of-three children's loss rose
    by about as much (seed 1: +0.0078), which means the children became more alike rather than the routing better.
    The BDT's own root regret moves between 0.150 and 0.185 from round to round with no split.
- Right after a split, R' routes like the old router. The regret the round before the split, then R' and each
  sub-router alone after it:
  - s1, round 4: before 0.1800; R' 0.1546, sub-routers 0.1541-0.1543.
  - s2, round 4: before 0.1900; R' 0.1515, sub-routers 0.1505-0.1519.
  - s2, round 6: before 0.1557; R' 0.1652, sub-routers 0.1650 each.
  The drop at round 4 is that round's drift: the BDT without the split went 0.1800 -> 0.1553 and 0.1900 -> 0.1509.

Q2. DO THE SUB-ROUTERS DIVERGE?
Slowly. A copy starts with all the counts of the router it was copied from (a CNBP router with forget factor 1 never
forgets). After that it trains only on the positions routed to it: bdd.rs trains only the routed child (train_node
recurses into children[routed]).
- Sibling agreement is the share of positions where two siblings reach the same leaf. One round after the first split
  it is 96.3% / 93.7% (s1 / s2), and 97.7% after s2's second split.
- Over the three BDDs' R' nodes with at least 2% of positions:
  - round 15: 40 nodes, median agreement 87.5% (45-100%); 12 of them send 96% or more to one sub-router.
  - round 60: 38 nodes, median 80% (28-99%); 6 of them do.
- Some stacks barely move. bdd-s2's nodes 23 and 27 and bddbal's nodes 25 and 30 were all made at round 14. At round
  60 each still sends 99-100% to one child, and its children still agree on 96-99% of its positions.

Q3. DOES R' LEARN TO USE THEM?
Not consistently.
- For each R' node, take its routed regret minus the regret of always using its most-used sub-router. "Stack" is the
  sum of that over all R' nodes per test position: what replacing each R' by its most-used sub-router, one node at a
  time, would change.
    round 15: s1 +0.0003 [-0.0007, +0.0013]   s2 +0.0003 [-0.0022, +0.0027]   bal +0.0018 [+0.0001, +0.0035]
    round 30: s1 -0.0021 [-0.0042, -0.0001]   s2 +0.0023 [-0.0005, +0.0052]   bal -0.0014 [-0.0037, +0.0009]
    round 60: s1 -0.0028 [-0.0052, -0.0005]   s2 +0.0056 [+0.0023, +0.0089]   bal -0.0014 [-0.0039, +0.0010]
- By round 60, seed 1's R' nodes gain 0.003 per position, mostly at node 13, which carries 84% of positions: -0.0021
  [-0.0037, -0.0005] at the node.
- Seed 2's lose 0.006, at nodes 7 and 3 (53% and 52% of positions): +0.0057 [+0.0024, +0.0089] and +0.0059 [+0.0012,
  +0.0106].
- The root's R' spreads its traffic (32/38/29 in s1, 39/30/31 in s2) over sub-routers that agree on 91-95% of
  positions, so it changes little either way.
- Held-out gives the same answer. BDD minus BDT-4 (the same 9 leaves, without router splits), mean of the two seeds:
  - round 20: -0.0016 [-0.0034, +0.0002]
  - round 40: +0.0007 [-0.0004, +0.0017]
  - round 60: +0.0010 [-0.0002, +0.0021]; winner log-loss +0.0010 [-0.0002, +0.0022] (per seed +0.0017 [+0.0000,
    +0.0033] and +0.0004 [-0.0010, +0.0018])
