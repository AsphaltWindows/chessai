    [-0.0097, -0.0002] / -0.0017 n.s.; Nc-share and Te n.s.; Ne v1878 0.012-0.021 worse.

    for Ne v1878 (the most distinct leaves, 0.19-0.22), smallest for Te (3 near-copies, 0.06-0.07). A loose ceiling.
  M-LP-L-c (Q1): a learned gate between its LR and probit experts does worse than its own gate (= the LR expert alone):
    0.008-0.050; the oracle between the two is 0.11-0.14 below.
  Per-region calibration (own routing, one calibration per region; leafcal-like): large offline gains:
    Nc-share -0.032 [-0.041, -0.022] / -0.001 n.s.; Ne -0.025 / -0.009; Ne-share -0.041 / -0.012;
    Te -0.032 / -0.009. Best leaf per region with its region calibration: a further 0.003-0.012 on d2play, none on s555.
  In play the per-leaf calibration hurts (leafcal: a calibration per leaf fitted on the own-cal games, against the
  league's single calibration on the same games; d1): Ne v1878 -43 [-53, -32], Nc-share -59 [-70, -49], Ne-share -93
  [-104, -83], Te -19 [-28, -10]; at d2 worse still: Ne v1878 -101 [-116, -86], Ne-share -147 [-163, -132].
  Where the offline gain sits: on d2play about half on decisive positions and half on draws, on s555 (93% decisive)
  mostly on draws; the decisive part includes the draw mass, so the win-vs-loss part, the only one play uses, was not
  separated. Ne v1878's per-leaf fits differ widely (winner slope 0.54-2.23, the whole model's 1.53), so moves whose
  positions land in different leaves are scored on different scales. Untested which part costs the Elo: the scales
  (temperature; see the calibration confound), the offsets, or the small own-play fit sets (143-30k positions a leaf).
  Conclusion: no realistic routing headroom found. The big offline gain from per-region calibration turns into a loss
  in play, so held-out log-loss is a poor guide to play strength here. Caveat: the flat routers saw 144k positions,
  the trees' routers 100M+ online, so the router test is a weak lower bound.
  Cross-set results (trained on s555, tested on d2play) are confounded by calibration transfer between sets with
  different draw rates (93% vs 75% decisive) and are set aside.

ANSWERS
  Q6 (why the good routed models win): at d2, where the league's top ratings are, only T-L-Nc-share-c v545 gains from its
    routing (40 over its best and busiest leaf). The other three champions play as one leaf: the busiest leaf alone is
    level with the tree (Ne v1878, Te-merge v833) or better (Ne-share v428, by 41). Those leaves are young (AdaGrad sums
    5-20% of F-L-c's). Youth vs filtered data is still open; the twin stream's leaf 2x2 tests it. The twin replay found
    the tree's lead over a flat LR at v122 is all data, consistent with this.
  Q4 (what routing works): NB (CNBP) routers and gates lead the league (T-L-Nc-share-c 1852, T-L-Ne-c 1844, M-L4-N-c
    1822); LR routers trail and LR gates collapse (moespec); TAN sits between, on two lineages. At d1 every routed tree
    beats its busiest leaf (26-69), so routing does real work at d1; at d2 that survives only for Nc-share.
  Q5 (how much better routing could give, the case for DAGs): no realistic headroom found. Learned flat routers never
    beat own routing; re-pointing regions to their best leaf gains at most 0.005 (Ne-share, d2play only); the oracle's
    0.06-0.22 is unreachable by any router tried. The big offline gain is per-region calibration, and it loses in play.
    Headroom gives no case for router re-splits in these models.
  Q1 (an LR+probit MoE): M-LP-L-c v62's gate gives its LR expert ~100%, so the mix is its LR. The starved probit expert
    alone beats the mix at d2 (44) and loses at d1 (43). A learned gate between the two does no better offline, and held-out
    log-loss favours the LR (on draws), so a gate trained on likelihood will keep starving the probit. A mix that helps at
    d2 needs a signal other than held-out log-loss.

CAVEATS
  - One version per lineage (champions, and M-LP-L-c's current); d2 versions swing 40-70 Elo (sharedrop), and champions
    are peaks.
  - Offline log-loss did not predict play anywhere here: per-region calibration, offline-best leaves (171 and 85 below
    their trees at d2), LR vs probit. Held-out log-loss includes draws, which the play policy ignores.
  - The learned routers saw 144k positions, the trees' own routers 100M+ online: "no headroom" bounds what a small router
    learns offline, not what a bigger one could.
  - A leaf alone is the leaf as the tree left it, never retrained alone.
  - leafcal was fitted on the own-cal games (600 d1 games, the league's way), the offline per-region fits on held-out sets.
  - The census confounds (league-data share, age, peaks) are untouched here; the solo lineages and the twin address them.

COST: about 1 h CPU: matches 30 min (measured), offline ~25 min (from wall time), builds ~5, own-cal sets ~1.
