lrprobit: is LR strictly better than probit, or is each better somewhere? And an LR + probit hybrid (2026-11-08)
User: "Another thing I would like you to look into is a potential probit-LR MoE investigation the way we did for
LR + NB." This is lrnb (models/from-scratch/lrnb/summary.txt) with the probit in NB's place, plus the hybrid itself,
built and staged at the coordinator's request.

VERDICT
LR is better than the probit everywhere we looked, as it was against NB. But the probit is not a weaker model of
everything: 97% of its deficit is the draw question. On who wins it is nearly level with LR.
- Log-loss: the probit is worse at every training size, from 10k positions (0.065 [0.053, 0.077]) to the full
  2.3M (0.140 [0.125, 0.155]). The gap grows with data, and there is no crossover.
- The loss splits exactly into "draw or decisive?" plus, on decisive positions, "who wins?". At full data the
  probit is 0.136 [0.122, 0.150] behind on the first and 0.0045 [0.0007, 0.0083] behind on the second. That is 97%
  of the gap in the draw question.
  - On the winner loss the learning curve crosses zero. It runs 0.013 at 10k positions, then -0.009 [-0.018, 0.001]
    at 100k, then 0.006 at 2.3M. None of the points favours the probit significantly.
- No group of positions has the probit ahead: it is worse in 50 of 59 label-free groups and ties in 9. Its worst
  places are drawish ones: bare endgames (2-6 pieces) 0.56 behind, games past ply 150 0.50-0.77 behind, drawn
  games 0.37 behind.
- The model's form explains this. An ordinal probit has one latent "white advantage" and one draw margin, so a
  draw can only mean "balanced". On these games its calibrated draw slope is negative (-0.35 to -0.40): where it
  expects draws, draws are rarer. LR has its own weights for the draw class.
- Gates: no gate beats LR alone, mixing or hard (section 3).
  - The best mixing gate, over both models' outputs, is -0.0011 [-0.0032, 0.0009] against LR alone (lrnb's NB
    gate: -0.0006).
  - Hard gates that pick by a mixing gate's weight hand the probit 0.01-1.2% of positions and score as LR does.
  - A hard gate trained to predict which model will do better hands the probit 17-19% of positions. The probit is
    in fact worse there, and the gate loses 0.0063 [0.0019, 0.0106].
  - The oracle's -0.108 is the noise bound lrnb found: over two LRs it "gains" 0.071.
- The hybrid (section 4) gains nothing offline either: -0.0014 [-0.0034, 0.0005] against LR alone. Its gate gives
  LR 98-99% of the weight, and the hard version hands the probit 0.4% of positions. Offline, both are LR with a
  little probit, so we expect them near F-L-c, at about 1.5x its cost. Their lines (MoE-LP-L-c, MoE-LP-L-hard-c)
  went to the coordinator anyway: the user's policy is that the league, not offline results, decides entry.
- League context: the league's play ranks moves by ln P(win) - ln P(loss), which ignores draws (player.rs, the
  log-odds policies). So the probit's big loss on draws never reaches its moves, and only the who-wins part bears
  on its strength. That fits the league:
  - flat probit trails flat LR (champions 1646 vs 1790), and its champion is behind on who wins: 0.008 [0.004,
    0.013];
  - at depth 2 the probit leads (1831 vs 1806), and the two champions tie on who wins: -0.001 [-0.007, 0.006].
  This is suggestive, not causal: the champions differ in age and data.

SETUP
lrnb's setup throughout, so the numbers compare.
- Data: offline-c15. Training is cycle 15's round-robins 80-83 (2,308,895 positions); the test set is round 84
  (575,099 positions in 10 parts).
- Models:
  - LR: F-L-c's recipe (lrnb's own lr-* models, linked into m/).
  - Probit: F-P-tau0-c's recipe (--model probit --probit-tau 0 --use-probs). It is trained with lrnb's seed and
    rounds, so it sees the LR's exact stream, on lrnb's nested subsets (data/sN) and on all of it.
- Binary: chessai-lrnb trains and scores the probit byte-identically to the league's chessai-probit (identity.out:
  models at 10k and 100k positions, and eval-loss on a test part). So no new build was needed for the analysis.
- Losses are held out twice, as in lrnb. Each model's own 4-parameter calibration is fitted on one fold (A = the
  even parts, B = the odd ones) and scored on the other.
  - 95% intervals: paired t over the 10 parts (9 df).
  - Groups use lrnb's ratio estimator with the parts as clusters.
  - The LR rows of curve.csv equal lrnb's byte for byte, and common.py's calibration reproduces the binary's
    per-part losses for the probit too.
- League models (m/league/SOURCE): the champions at cycle 41 of F-L-c (v4100), F-P-tau0-c (v3595), F-L-d2jit-c
  (v4978) and F-P-tau0-d2jit-c (v5150), copied and calibrated the same way.

- The Bayesian prior doesn't help early: the probit is furthest behind in relative terms at 10k positions, and
  NB tied LR there (lrnb). From 30k positions on, it flattens toward 0.76 while LR keeps improving.
- Side note: the default tau (1e-5) at full data differs from tau 0 by -0.0007 [-0.0021, 0.0006] (log-loss) and
  -0.0010 [-0.0024, 0.0003] (winner). That is a tie.
- Calibration (eval_curve.out; fold A / fold B). The first parameter is the slope on the model's white-vs-black
  log-odds; the second is the slope on its decisive-vs-draw log-odds.
  - lr-all: 1.02 / 1.05 and 1.03 / 0.98.
  - p-all: 1.07 / 1.12 and -0.40 / -0.35.
  - The league champions:
    - F-L-c: 1.21 / 1.24 and 1.81 / 1.72.
    - F-P-tau0-c: 1.08 / 1.09 and 1.22 / 0.58.
    - F-L-d2jit-c: 0.94 / 0.95 and 1.33 / 1.26.
    - F-P-tau0-d2jit-c: 1.20 / 1.22 and -1.25 / -1.15.
  The probit's own draw signal points the wrong way on these games, offline and in the depth-2 league champion.

3. GATE (gate.py, gate.out; lrnb's gates plus hard picks)
Each part is scored by a gate fitted on the other fold. Both calibrations and the gate are fitted on that fold too.
The mix is p = g LR + (1-g) P; the hard gates pick one model per position. The settings were fixed before any
held-out loss was seen.

     method        held-out log-loss  minus LR alone [95% CI]   winner  minus LR [95% CI]
     LR alone      0.6234                                         0.3299
     probit alone  0.7635   0.1401 [ 0.1249,  0.1553]             0.3357   0.0059 [ 0.0009,  0.0108]
     constant      0.6236   0.0002 [-0.0002,  0.0005]             0.3299  -0.0000 [-0.0001,  0.0001]
     outputs       0.6222  -0.0011 [-0.0032,  0.0009]             0.3296  -0.0003 [-0.0005, -0.0001]
     full gate     0.6229  -0.0005 [-0.0032,  0.0023]             0.3295  -0.0004 [-0.0008,  0.0001]
     hard-outputs  0.6234   0.0000 [ 0.0000,  0.0000]             0.3299   0.0000 [-0.0000,  0.0000]
     hard-full     0.6236   0.0002 [-0.0002,  0.0006]             0.3298  -0.0001 [-0.0002,  0.0001]
     hard-reg      0.6297   0.0063 [ 0.0019,  0.0106]             0.3303   0.0004 [-0.0003,  0.0011]
     oracle        0.5150  -0.1084 [-0.1193, -0.0976]             0.2939  -0.0360 [-0.0403, -0.0316]

- The mixing gates:
  - constant: one weight for every position. The fitted weight on LR is 0.995 on fold A and 0.972 on fold B
    (lrnb's NB: 0.992 and 0.925).
  - outputs: a sigmoid over both models' calibrated log-probabilities and confidences. Its held-out weight on LR
    averages 0.97 and 0.94.
  - full gate: outputs plus the 70 features one-hot (851 weights). It cut its own fold's training loss by
    0.003-0.006 more than outputs did, and none of that holds out.
  - The only interval clear of zero is the outputs gate's on the winner loss: 0.0003 [0.0001, 0.0005]. That is
    negligible.
- The hard gates (the user prefers hard routing):
  - hard-outputs and hard-full: the probit wherever the matching mixing gate gives it more than half the weight.
    They hand it 0.01-0.6% and 0.1-1.2% of held-out positions and score as LR does.
  - hard-reg: a least-squares regression of the probit's loss minus LR's (clipped to 5 nats either way) on the full
    gate's inputs, with the gates' Adagrad settings. It picks the probit where the predicted difference is below
    zero.
    - It explains 17-18% of the variance on its own fold and hands the probit 17-19% of held-out positions.
    - Those are not positions where the probit is better: its loss there is about 0.036 a position above LR's,
      hence the loss of 0.0063.
  - The hard hybrid (section 4) hands the probit 0.38% of test positions, 2,207 of 575,099 (hybrid/routed.out).
- The oracle is the same loose bound as in lrnb. It picks the better model after seeing the result.
  - The same oracle over two LRs, lr-all and the league's F-L-c, "gains" 0.071 (oracle_check.out). Their most
    likely results differ on 12% of positions.
  - Over LR and the probit it gains 0.108, and their most likely results differ on 25%. Over the depth-2
    champions it gains 0.122, with 26%.
  - So the larger gain mostly reflects more disagreement, not a probit that is right more often.

4. THE HYBRID (hybrid/; built after the coordinator's 21:00 message: league entry is not gated on offline results)
Binary bin/chessai-hybrid, md5 89605f852f5dfeee72b32218b35d5b5f.
- Source: models/from-scratch/hybrid-src = lrnb-src (sparsefit-src plus eval-loss --dump) plus src/hybrid.rs and
  its wiring in lib.rs, classifier.rs and main.rs (hybrid/hybrid.patch).
- Tests: 140 pass, 5 of them new.
- --model hybrid --hybrid-experts lr,probit (generic over lr, probit and cnbp, 2 to 4 experts).
  - Each expert trains on every position exactly as it would alone.
  - The gate is a logistic regression over the position with one class per expert. It starts uniform and learns
    the experts' responsibilities from their predictions before they learn (moe.rs's gate step).
  - It predicts with the mixture. --hybrid-hard predicts with the gate's top expert alone.
  - --hybrid-pin K predicts with expert K alone, for identity checks.
  - The options live in the model file, so --resume and --start keep them.
  - NB experts work (a CNBP expert's training-time predictions come from its counts), but no NB line was
    prepared, as the user abandoned NB as a predictor.
- Identity (hybrid/identity.out), byte for byte:
  - lr, probit and cnbp train as chessai-sparsefit does.
  - F-L-c's self-play recipe runs as chessai-cyc5's, and so does the hybrid pinned to its LR: models v1-3,
    ladder.csv, results.txt. Pinned to its probit, it runs as chessai-probit's F-P-tau0-c recipe.
  - Unpinned, its experts equal the standalone models trained on the same stream: at 100k positions, and at full
    data (h-all's experts are lr-all and p-all).
  - 3 rounds straight equal 2 rounds plus --resume.
  - The driver's commands work: the own step's train --start, the gate's arena, eval-loss, cal: specs, mix-stats.
- Tournament binary. The league's tournament and own-cal games must load hybrid:PATH, so the coordinator switches
  the tournament binary to chessai-hybrid. With the live flags, chessai-sparsefit and chessai-hybrid wrote
  identical files:
  - 1,033 files on lr, probit, cnbp, bdt (NB and TAN routers; the LR-router entry picked had left the field), bdd,
    moe and nbgate entries (tourney.out);
  - 2,553 files on fm, ht with LR and NB leaves, tan, moenb, nbmix (ply 1 and a frozen ply-2 champion), the random
    references (depths 0-3), the d4mix reference and the frozen F-P-c (tourney2.out);
  - each with a retirement and late joiners.
- The hard line's smoke (hard-smoke.out): self-play, exact --resume, the driver's own step (the hard flag kept),
  and the gate arena.
- Offline (hybrid/offline.out, summary.out), held out against lr-all:
  - hybrid: log-loss -0.0014 [-0.0034, 0.0005], winner -0.0001 [-0.0002, 0.0001];
  - hard: -0.0003 [-0.0014, 0.0007], winner 0.0000.
  - Its gate's mean weight on LR over the test parts is 0.98-0.99, and above 0.99 on 70-79% of positions. In
    effect it is LR.
  - The hard version hands the probit 0.07-1.2% of each part's positions, 0.38% in all (hybrid/routed.sh).
- Cost: 3 x 100 self-play games at depth 0 took 1.61 s unpinned against 1.09 s pinned to its LR (which plays as
  F-L-c does), so about 1.5x F-L-c's CPU per game: it evaluates both experts and the gate.
- Lines sent to the coordinator (one factor each; MoE-LP-L-c's comparator is F-L-c, and the hard twin's is
  MoE-LP-L-c):
    MoE-LP-L-c | MoE-LP-L | models/from-scratch/bin/chessai-hybrid | scratch | lr | own | --model hybrid
      --hybrid-experts lr,probit --probit-tau 0 --use-probs --seed 21 --vs-previous --no-games
    MoE-LP-L-hard-c: the same plus --hybrid-hard.
  Both need own_games.binary_by_lineage set to chessai-hybrid.

CAVEATS
- This is prediction loss on league games (round 84), not playing strength, and the league plays by win/loss
  log-odds.
- One global calibration per model, as in lrnb.
- The league pairs are champions of different ages, trained on their own self-play data.
- The oracle is a loose bound: it picks after seeing the result (section 3).

CPU: about 22 CPU-minutes in all, at nice 19 with at most 2 jobs.
- The analysis: about 3.5 minutes.
  - Training 9 s, eval_curve 36 s, dumps 12 s, groups 25 s, decompose 15 s, oracle_check 16 s.
  - The gate study 80 s.
- 13 minutes were wasted on a first gate run with a quadratic diagnostic print. It was killed and fixed.
- The hybrid's builds and tests: about 3 minutes.
- Its identity, tournament and smoke checks: about 2 minutes. Its offline run and routed count: 25 s.
- The dumps (690 MB) are deleted after use; dump.sh regenerates them in about a minute.
