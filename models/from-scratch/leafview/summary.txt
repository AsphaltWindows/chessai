leafview: what the leaves of T-L-Ne-share3-c and T-L-Ne-fresh-c look like (2026-11-09)

Analysis only. Nothing was deployed, cycles/ was only read, rs/ was not touched. The models were copied into models/
first.

Pages (self-contained, open from disk, light and dark):
  share3.html: T-L-Ne-share3-c v394, cycle 71's champion
  fresh.html:  T-L-Ne-fresh-c v1659, the champion since cycle 65
Each page has: structure (tree diagram, when splits and merges happened), rating history, routing (shares, each leaf's
positions in chess terms, what each router looks at), leaf weights as piece-square boards (any leaf or the mean leaf;
win-loss or draw; value, positional, or minus the mean leaf; numbers on request), effective piece values, the
non-square features, leaf-to-leaf correlations, how differently the leaves predict, and the recipe in plain words.

Positions: 1,280,370 from 14,915 league games of cycle 70's pool (5 tournaments, every 16th pairing). The chess-terms
tables leave out the 26,460 positions from games with a random player.


1. T-L-Ne-share3-c v394 (chessai-sparsefit, leaf share 0.3)

- Shape: 11 leaves under 5 routers, depth up to 4. Two leaves take three quarters of league positions:
    L8   40.6%  middlegame, median ply 53
    L7   33.9%  opening, median ply 16, a castling right in 98%
    L15  10.1%  long endgames, median ply 134, 55% from drawn games
    L4    5.6%  endgames (58% of its positions come from games white won)
    L5    4.7%  endgames (66% from games black won)
  The other six leaves get under 2% each.
- The leaves are near-copies.
  - Two leaves' win-loss weights correlate at a median of 0.9989 (lowest 0.9943). Draw weights: 0.9947.
  - Their winner log-odds on league positions correlate at 0.9992.
  - The routed leaf differs from the other leaves' mean by a median of 0.13 nats; positions spread over an SD of
    3.1 nats. The two disagree on who is better in 2.6% of positions.
- Why they can be so alike. This is from the code; it was not tested here.
  - A split copies the leaf into each child, AdaGrad sums too (forget factor 1).
  - Leaf share 0.3 trains every leaf on every other leaf's positions at 0.3 weight. With league routing, a leaf's own
    positions would be this much of its training weight: L8 70%, L7 63%, L15 27%, L4 17%, L5 14%, the six small
    leaves 1-6%. Self-play routes differently, so these are estimates.
- T-L-Ne-share-c v618 (leaf share 0.1, not rated yet) has less alike leaves: median 0.9961 (lowest 0.9854); draw
  0.9836.
- The weights (mean leaf, white's pieces, log-odds against an empty square): queen 2.12 (F-L-c 1.76), rook 1.01,
  bishop 0.74, knight 0.53, pawn 0.48.
  - Pawns are worth most on the 7th rank.
  - Queens are best in the centre (e4, e5).
  - The white king is best on e6/d6/f6 (an advanced king) and worst in the far corners.
  - Black's pieces mirror this.
- Rating: 1889.9 ± 20.4 at cycle 71, 58 above the mean of the ten versions before it (1831.8, SD 22.1). One version
  swings about 17 Elo (SD), so part of the 58 is likely luck.
- Splits: the five splits (v27-46, v123-133, v207-217, v267-274, v366-371; cycles 29-67) came while the rating
  climbed steadily. The history can't separate them from training.


2. T-L-Ne-fresh-c v1659 (chessai-resid4, --revise 2 --revise-on fresh --reset-leaves inf)

The flags in plain words (from chessai-resid4 --help and models/from-scratch/resid-src):
- Shadow: each router keeps one, the leaf it was before it split. The shadow goes on training on every position that
  passes through the router.
- --revise 2: the test runs after every 5M examples through a router.
  - It compares the branch below the router with the shadow on the log-loss of who won (decisive positions only).
  - If the branch's mean gain over the shadow is more than 2 standard errors below zero, the branch is merged back:
    the shadow becomes a leaf again.
  - The undone splits stop counting towards the limit of 5, so the tree can split elsewhere.
- --revise-on fresh: the test scores each self-play round's positions with the model as it was at the start of the
  round, before it trains on them. Neither the branch nor the shadow has seen those positions. The alternative,
  stream, scores each position as training reaches it.
- --reset-leaves inf: the same test for single leaves against their router's shadow. With inf it only compares and
  logs; it never resets.
- The other flags are as in share3, except that there is no leaf share: each leaf trains only on its own positions.

Findings:
- Shape: 11 leaves under 5 routers, the same in v1659 and in the latest version, v1714.
    L2   60.6%  openings into the early middlegame, median ply 27, a castling right in 80%
    L1   19.8%  middlegames, median ply 69
    L9   10.3%  endgames, median ply 98
    L13   6.4%  late endgames, median ply 142, 58% from drawn games
    L15   2.2%
  The other six leaves get under 0.5% each.
  - L7 gets none of the 1,280,370 positions and L8 gets 20. Router 4 sends almost every league position to L9. L7 and L8
    have trained on only 19,664 and 3,734 examples since they were made at v525-566.
- The structure was revised once. The evidence is the state sizes and the models either side of each change
  (bdt1486/1487, bdt1640/1641).
  - The first run made all five splits by v566.
  - Between v1486 and v1487 the test merged router 2's branch (five leaves, two splits) back into its shadow. That
    shadow is today's L2: the leaf node 2 was at its split (v165-219), trained since on every position through it.
  - In the same step leaf 8 (113.5M examples) split; it is now router 5.
  - At v1640-1641 router 5's leaf 12 split.
- The leaves differ far more than share3's.
  - Win-loss weights correlate at a median of 0.9860 (lowest 0.9474). Draw weights: 0.9287 (lowest 0.8225).
  - The routed leaf differs from the others' mean by a median of 0.53 nats, against 0.13 for share3.
  - The two disagree on who is better in 13.3% of positions, against 2.6% for share3.
- The specialised leaves are worse outside their region. Log-loss of who won, on decisive league positions:
    the tree             0.3818
    L2 everywhere        0.3835
    endgame leaves       0.41-0.46
    F-L-c                0.3911
- The weights:
  - The late-endgame leaves under router 5 (L10, L11, L13-L15) value a pawn at about 0.85. The other leaves value it
    at 0.32-0.58; F-L-c at 0.49.
  - The mean leaf values the queen at 1.71, close to F-L-c's 1.76.
  - The board patterns match share3's.
- Rating: v1659 was rated 1864.7 ± 20.1 as the field version at cycle 65 (the ten versions before averaged 1819.4,
  SD 15.1). It has been the champion since: 1855.5 ± 7.6 at cycle 71.
  - v1714, the latest version, is not rated yet. The 1819.8 ± 19.4 at cycle 71 belongs to v1705.
- The merge falls between cycles 44 and 45. Fresh's rating above the league median went from 67 (cycles 40-44) to
  108 (cycles 45-49). That is one event, against noise of about 17 Elo per version, so it does not show that the
  merge helped.
- From v1659 to v1714 (55 versions), every leaf's win-loss weights still correlate at 0.9989 or more. L13 and L14's
  shares moved most: 6.4% to 4.8%, and 0.2% to 1.4%.


3. The two compared

- Same size: 11 leaves, 5 routers, depth up to 4.
- How positions are spread:
  - share3 splits the opening and the middlegame between two big leaves (L7 34%, L8 41%).
  - fresh has one leaf for the opening and early middlegame (L2, 61%; it is the merged shadow) and puts more of its
    small leaves deep in endgames.
- Similarity:
  - share3's leaves are near-copies.
  - fresh's are distinct: their predictions differ about 4 times as much (0.53 nats against 0.13), and they disagree
    on who is better 5 times as often.
  - A share3 leaf and a fresh leaf correlate at a median of 0.974 (win-loss) and 0.911 (draw), so the two models
    share broad patterns.
  - Each tree's leaves correlate with the flat F-L-c at 0.985-0.994 (share3) and 0.97-0.99 (fresh).
- How much the leaves have trained: AdaGrad sums of the bias, relative to F-L-c's, are 0.02-0.055 for share3's
  leaves and 0.05-0.18 for fresh's. Both trees' leaves are much younger than F-L-c and still take larger steps.
- Piece values: share3's mean leaf values the queen higher (2.12 against fresh's 1.71 and F-L-c's 1.76). fresh
  values pawns higher (0.63 against 0.48), most of all in its endgame leaves.
- Prediction, measured on the same league positions:
  - log-loss of who won: share3's tree 0.3801, fresh's 0.3818, F-L-c 0.3911;
  - neither tree beats its own best single leaf by more than about 0.002, which is within noise for positions
    clustered in games.
  These are raw probabilities. The league plays with calibrated values, and earlier studies found that offline loss
  does not predict play. They don't rank the models.
- What this does not show: whether near-copy leaves (share3) or distinct leaves (fresh) play better. The two
  lineages differ in more than that: binary, leaf share, the revise test, age, and the merge. Each is one lineage.


Caveats

- The correlations are over the 820 weights F-L-c ever updated: raw win-loss (w0-w1) and draw (w2-(w0+w1)/2)
  contrasts, all features.
- The boards are gauge-fixed: a piece on a square against that square empty.
- The prediction comparisons use white's winner log-odds, which is what play uses.
  - The three-class log-loss is not used. Both lineages' recent self-play is 91% draws (last 50 rounds), so the
    models give a draw 83-85% on average on league positions, of which 13.5% come from drawn games. That
    mismatch dominates the three-class loss.
  - share-c v618 gives a draw 17%.
- Splits and merges are dated from the sizes of the cycle states, which only resolve cycles. The models either side
  of fresh's two changes were read to pin those down.
- Rating comparisons: one version swings about 17 Elo and windows of versions drift about 30. Nothing here is a
  controlled test.
