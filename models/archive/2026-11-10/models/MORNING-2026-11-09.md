## The short version
1. The trees' league edge is mostly data, not routing. On exactly T-L-Ne-share-c's games, league games included,
   a flat LR plays level with the tree or beats it at depth 2: -5 and 14 at v122 and v217 (single versions), and
   -26 [-42, -10] averaged over v444-v452 (one window of versions).
2. League games are worth far more per position than self-play games: about 300 Elo from 10% of the positions.
   Every lineage gets the same ~400k league positions a cycle, so slow lineages are mostly league-trained:
   depth-2 self-play 65-91% of a cycle's positions, depth 4 99%, trees and MoEs 15-26%, flat depth 0 5-6%.
   This flatters slow architectures in the ratings. It needs your decision (below).
3. On equal data LR beats probit, and at depth 2 the gap grows with data (-10, -50, -101). The step size isn't
   the problem: tau makes the probit worse, larger LR rates lose, and smaller ones (0.025, 0.0166) lose early and
   are level with F-L-c's 0.05 later. 0.05 looks near a flat optimum.
4. Routing to the "right" leaf: no evidence it matters. T-L-Ne-share-c's busiest leaf, L9, is not clearly better
   than a flat LR on the same data (18 [-6, 43]), nor than the same data weighting on random positions
   (5 [-17, 26]).
5. Of the four champion trees checked, only T-L-Nc-share-c's routing helped in play at depth 2 (40, one version).
   No learnable routing headroom was found.
6. The tournament runs on a faster, exact binary since cycle 51: -23% CPU like for like; cycles take 42 min, not 60.
7. Caveat: a model's playing strength swings about 17 Elo (SD) between adjacent versions, twice the match noise,
   and it also drifts over tens of versions: a contrast averaged over one nine-version window moved by ~30 in the
   next. So contrasts of 20-40 Elo need several separate windows. Three readings I sent you overnight fell:
   "routing to L9's region is worth 23", the "~80 Elo interaction" of data and step, and "rate 0.025 beats 0.05
   by 31". Effects of 100+ Elo stand (league data, probit vs LR, larger rates losing).

## Q2. Saturation: does a shrinking step hold models back?
- LR: no. Larger rates lose on equal data at every checkpoint (tree minus twin at v122, v217, v452: rate 0.10
  41, 57, 18; rate 0.165 99, 130, 82; F-L-c's 0.05 -5, 14, and -26 averaged over v444-v452). In the league,
  F-L-agdiv-c (F-L-c v4725 with its AdaGrad sums divided by 2.7, a 1.64x step) against F-L-c at the same age:
  9, -20, -8, -11, -29 over cycles 48-52. No gain; if anything slightly worse, as the rate ladder below predicts.
- Probit: no. Tau keeps its step from shrinking, and it makes depth-2 play worse (table above).
- A smaller step doesn't help either. Flat twins on the same stream, Elo against F-L-c's rate 0.05 at depth 2:
| rate | v122 | v217 | v424-v432 averaged | v444-v452 averaged |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.025 | -25 | -2 | 31 [20, 43] | 2 [-11, 14] |
| 0.0166 | -69 | -26 | 15 [0, 30] | -5 [-28, 18] |
  - Smaller rates lose early. Later the first window favoured 0.025, but the next window doesn't replicate it, so
    playing strength drifts over tens of versions, not only between neighbours. No case for changing F-L-c's rate:
    with F-L-agdiv-c's bigger step also no better, 0.05 looks near a flat optimum.
  - A ~1.7x step on all data loses 46 (Q6), and the larger-rate twins lose at every checkpoint.

## Decisions for you
1. League-data share. Options:
   a. Cap each lineage's own step at a common share of its cycle's training (for example F-L-c's ~6%). Then the
      league compares architectures on equal data mixes. Slow lineages will drop a lot.
   b. Keep it, and read slow lineages' ratings as "architecture plus league data".
   c. Train the own step on the depth-2 entry's games only. That halves the intake and keeps its value (190 of
      221 Elo at v122, 202 of 212 at v217, 307 against 302 at v452). It narrows the gap without removing it.
   My recommendation: (a) if the league's job is to judge architectures, (b) if it is to find the strongest system.
2. The Q7 renames.
3. Incremental (NNUE-style) sums: maybe another -30% of tournament CPU, but games are no longer byte-identical
   unless the sums go fixed-point.
4. The same fast paths for self-play: more games a cycle, unevenly (depth-2 lineages gain 20-45%, depth-0 trees
   less). That changes the experiment's conditions once.
5. The queue, which waits for ~60 active: M-LP-N-c, M-L4-T-c, T-L-Ne-share-solo-c, T-P-Ne-share-c, and
   F-P-tau0-agdiv-c. I'd drop F-P-tau0-agdiv-c: F-L-agdiv-c gained nothing, and tau made the probit worse.
6. A second window for the headline (tree vs flat twin over v432-v440, ~10 CPU min), to firm up the "-26".
   Optional: the conclusion that the tree's league edge was data doesn't depend on it.
